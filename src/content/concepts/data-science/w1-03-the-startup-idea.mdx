The case study starts from a real research paper: *Efficient Neural Music Generation*
(Lam et al., NeurIPS 2023). It presents **MeLoDy**, a system that generates music of
state-of-the-art quality while cutting the forward passes needed by 95.7% to 99.6% compared
with the earlier MusicLM, for 10 to 30 seconds of music.

You do not need the architecture to follow the case. The point is what the paper does and
does not claim: it is faster and cheaper at comparable quality.

## The scenario

Put yourself in this position:

- You have just finished a Master's or PhD thesis on making deep learning for generative AI
  **more energy efficient**.
- Your case study was music generation. You showed **30% energy savings** with similar
  quality on benchmark prompts.
- Two friends are excited about the application.
- Their idea: *commercialise it and sell it to end users or music producers.*

## Research success versus product success

Everything above is a result about a model on a fixed set of prompts. It is exactly what a
thesis is supposed to deliver, and it is a good result.

A product asks a different question: does a **customer** get something they value, every
time, at a price you can sustain? None of the slides so far say anything about that. The
audience is invited to notice the gap before it is spelled out.

> A model that wins on a benchmark has answered a research question. A product has to
> answer a customer's.
