All the pieces are in place: an update rule driven by surprise, and an S-shaped curve to
turn a rating gap into a probability. The slides now run real numbers through both.

| player | rating before |
| --- | --- |
| Magnus Carlsen (A) | 2840 |
| Hikaru Nakamura (B) | 2700 |

## A change of convention

This example scores a **win as 1 and a loss as 0**, where the earlier slide used `+1` and
`−1`. The idea is the same. The practical effect is that the expected score `E_A` is now
simply A's probability of winning. This is the convention chess uses.

## Step 1: the expected score

```
E_A = 1 / (1 + 10^((R_B − R_A) / 400))
E_B = 1 − E_A
```

This is the curve from the last concept with two adjustments for chess: base 10 in place of
`e`, and the difference divided by 400 so that ratings in the thousands give sensible
probabilities. A 400-point gap corresponds to ten-to-one odds.

The slide substitutes `(2700 − 2840)/400` and reports `E_A = 0.76`, `E_B = 0.24`:
**Magnus is expected to win 76% of the time, Hikaru only 24%.**

## Step 2: the result and the update

Magnus **loses**. So `S_A = 0` and `S_B = 1`, and with `K = 20`:

```
R′_A = 2840 + 20 · (0 − 0.76) = 2825
R′_B = 2700 + 20 · (1 − 0.24) = 2715
```

| player | new rating | change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Magnus Carlsen | 2825 | −15 |
| Hikaru Nakamura | 2715 | +15 |

## Check the arithmetic yourself

Work the exponent by hand: `(2700 − 2840)/400 = −0.35`, and `10^−0.35 ≈ 0.447`, so
`E_A = 1 / 1.447 ≈ 0.69`. The slide's 0.76 is what the formula gives for a gap of 200
points, not 140.

| | `E_A` | Magnus | Hikaru |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| As printed on the slide | 0.76 | 2840 − 15.2 → 2825 | 2700 + 15.2 → 2715 |
| Evaluating the formula for 140 points | 0.69 | 2840 − 13.8 → 2826 | 2700 + 13.8 → 2714 |

The method and the conclusion are the same in both rows. If you are asked to reproduce a
calculation like this, show the exponent step so that a marker can follow your arithmetic.

## What to notice

- **The changes are equal and opposite.** What Magnus loses, Hikaru gains. Ratings are
  passed between the two players, and none are created.
- **The upset cost more than half of `K`.** Had they been evenly matched, each expected
  score would be 0.5 and the swing would be `20 × 0.5 = 10`. Magnus was the favourite, so
  losing costs him more.
- **Had Magnus won**, he would have gained `20 × (1 − 0.76) ≈ 5`. For a favourite, a win
  gains less than a loss costs.
- **`K` caps the damage.** No single match can move a rating by more than 20, so one bad day
  does not erase a career.

> Expected score from the rating gap, then rating plus `K` times actual minus expected. That
> is the whole Elo system.
