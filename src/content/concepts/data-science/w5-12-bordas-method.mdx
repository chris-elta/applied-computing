Four friends each write down their order of preference for five restaurants. The lists
disagree. You need one list. How do you combine them?

The slide frames the same problem two ways.

- **An election.** Determine the winner of a multiparty election where each voter ranks the
  candidates in order of preference: 1. BCIT, 2. MIT, 3. Illinois and so on.
- **Features.** Merge rankings independently drawn on different features. Rank laptops by
  price, by battery life and by weight, then combine the three orderings.

In the second framing each feature acts as a voter. This approach also avoids the
normalization problem: once every feature has been turned into ranks, price in dollars and
battery life in hours are on the same footing.

## Borda's method

**By assigning an increasing score per position, the resulting point total ranks the
items.** First place scores 1, second scores 2 and so on. Add up each item's scores across
all voters and sort by the total.

The slide's example has four voters, each ranking five items. Each column is one voter's
ballot, read from top to bottom.

| position | voter 1 | voter 2 | voter 3 | voter 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A | B | A | A |
| 2 | C | A | B | B |
| 3 | B | C | C | D |
| 4 | D | D | E | C |
| 5 | E | E | D | E |

Now find each item on every ballot and add its positions.

| item | positions | total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 1 + 2 + 1 + 1 | **5** |
| B | 3 + 1 + 2 + 2 | **8** |
| C | 2 + 3 + 3 + 4 | **12** |
| D | 4 + 4 + 5 + 3 | **16** |
| E | 5 + 5 + 4 + 5 | **19** |

The lowest total wins, so the merged ranking is A, B, C, D, E.

## Which way the points run

Here a **low** total is good, because first place earns 1. Many descriptions of Borda's
method score the other way, with first place earning the most points and a high total being
good. Both give the same order. When you read someone's Borda totals, check which way the
points run before you name the winner.

## Why not count first-place votes?

Counting first places uses one line of each ballot and discards the rest. Borda uses all of
it. An item that many voters place second, and nobody places last, can beat an item that
some voters love and others put at the bottom. Borda favours broad acceptability over
intense support from a few.

That may or may not be what you want. It is a design choice, like the others this week, and
there are cases where different methods name different winners.

> To merge rankings, give each position a score and add them up. Every line of every ballot
> counts.
