A doctor wants to know whether a lump is cancerous. One way is a biopsy: cut out a sample
and examine it. That is slow, expensive and unpleasant, and its answer is trusted. Another
way is a blood marker that tends to be raised in patients with cancer. That is quick and
cheap, and sometimes wrong.

The slide gives the two kinds of answer names.

| | definition | examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gold standard** | Labels or answers we trust to be correct, reflecting the scoring goal | A biopsy result |
| **Proxy** | An available quantity correlated with what we want to measure | A blood marker; a step count; a GPA |

The slide's own example is aimed at you: **your GPA or SAT/GRE is a proxy for how you
should do in my class.** The instructor cannot observe how you will do. What exists is a
number from your past that tends to go along with future performance. It tends to, and that
is all.

## Two conditions for a gold standard

Read the definition closely. It has two parts.

1. **Trusted to be correct.** You would settle an argument by looking it up.
2. **Reflecting the scoring goal.** It is the thing you care about, not a neighbour of it.

A measurement can pass the first and fail the second. A step count from a good sensor is
very accurate about steps. If your goal is fitness, it is still a proxy, because steps are
not fitness. Accuracy does not make a proxy into a gold standard.

## Why proxies are everywhere

For most things worth scoring, the gold standard is hard, slow or expensive to get, or does
not exist. There is no place to look up a student's true understanding, a team's true
strength or a web page's true importance. So you measure what is available and hope it
moves with the goal.

That is a reasonable thing to do. The scores earlier this week are all built from proxies:
BMI stands in for body fat, a transcript score for ability. The risk comes when you forget
which kind of number you are holding.

## How a proxy fails

A proxy is tied to the goal only by correlation, and correlation can break.

- **In a different population.** BMI tracks body fat in most people and misreads muscular
  athletes.
- **When people aim at it.** Shaking your wrist adds steps without adding fitness. Once a
  proxy is rewarded, people can raise it without raising the goal.

In both cases the proxy keeps producing values that look as reliable as before.

> A gold standard is what you want to know. A proxy is what you can measure. Be clear about
> which one a number is before you rely on it.
