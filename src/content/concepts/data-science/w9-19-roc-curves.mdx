Every metric so far is measured at **one threshold**. Move the threshold and precision,
recall and F all change. An ROC curve shows the classifier at **every** threshold at once.

## Building the curve

From the lecture notes, two rates:

```
TPR = TP / (TP + FN)        true positive rate: the same as recall
FPR = FP / (FP + TN)        false positive rate: negatives wrongly flagged
```

Now sweep the threshold from the top to the bottom of the score range, and plot one point
(FPR, TPR) at each setting.

| threshold | what happens | point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| above every score | nothing is called positive | (0, 0) |
| moving down | more of both groups pass the line | the curve rises and moves right |
| below every score | everything is called positive | (1, 1) |

Slide 76 draws the result beside the two score distributions it came from. The **Receiver
Operating Characteristic** curve (the slide titles it "Receiver-Operator") is the path from
(0, 0) to (1, 1).

## Reading it

The shape tells you how well the scores separate the classes:

- **A good classifier** bows toward the top-left corner. As the threshold drops, it gains
  positives (TPR climbs) long before it starts flagging negatives (FPR stays low).
- **A random classifier** runs along the **diagonal**. Each step catches as large a share of
  negatives as positives.
- **A perfect classifier** goes straight up the left edge to (0, 1), then across. Some
  threshold separates the groups completely.

## Area under the curve

The **AUC** turns the curve into one number. From the lecture notes: a random classifier has
AUC = 0.5, a perfect one has AUC = 1.0, and "the larger the area under the curve, the better
the model's discriminatory power". Slide 76 calls it "a measure of accuracy"; the notes'
word, *discrimination*, is more precise. AUC measures how well the scores rank positives above
negatives, across all thresholds, before you have committed to one.

That is its strength and its limit. AUC compares *models*. Once you deploy, you still choose
one threshold, and then the precision-recall trade-off from the last concept decides whether
that point on the curve suits the application.

> An ROC curve plots true positive rate against false positive rate at every threshold. Hugging
> the top-left is good; the diagonal is guessing. AUC sums it up from 0.5 for chance to 1.0 for
> perfect.
