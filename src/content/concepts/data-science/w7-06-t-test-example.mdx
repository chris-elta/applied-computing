This is the t-test from the previous concept, worked through with the slides' numbers.

## The scenario

You want to know whether a new studying technique improves exam performance, and test it on
two small groups:

| group | `n` | mean `x̄` | standard deviation `s` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| traditional | 10 | 75 | 5 |
| new technique | 10 | 80 | 4 |

## Step 1 · State the hypotheses

- `H₀`: no difference in mean scores, `μ₁ = μ₂`.
- `H₁`: there is a difference, `μ₁ ≠ μ₂`.

The alternative allows either direction, so this is a **two-tailed** test.

## Step 2 · Compute Welch's t

Each group contributes `s²/n` to the noise:

| | `s²` | `s²/n` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| traditional | `5² = 25` | `25/10 = 2.5` |
| new technique | `4² = 16` | `16/10 = 1.6` |
| sum | | `4.1` |

```
t = (75 − 80) / √4.1 = −5 / 2.025 ≈ −2.47
```

The slide rounds `√4.1` to 2.05 and gets −2.44. The exact value is about −2.47, and the
conclusion is the same either way.

The sign only says that the traditional group scored lower. For a two-tailed test, compare
`|t| ≈ 2.47`.

## Step 3 · Degrees of freedom

Using the slides' approximation:

```
df ≈ n₁ + n₂ − 2 = 10 + 10 − 2 = 18
```

## Step 4 · Look up the critical value

In a t-table, find the row `df = 18` and the column for `α = 0.05`, two-tailed. The value is
`t₀.₀₅(18) ≈ 2.10`.

## Step 5 · Decide

`2.47 > 2.10`, so **the difference is significant at the 0.05 level**. Reject `H₀`. In the
slide's words, the new technique improved scores enough that the difference is *unlikely due to
random variation*.

## What this does and does not say

- It says a 5-point gap between groups of ten is bigger than chance usually produces, given
  this much spread.
- It does not say how much better the technique is in general. The true improvement could be
  smaller or larger than 5. That is an effect-size question.
- It does not survive a stricter standard. At `α = 0.01`, the critical value for 18 degrees of
  freedom is about 2.88, and 2.47 falls short. Where you set `α` matters, and it must be set
  before you look.

Notice how close this was. With the same means and spreads but only five students per group,
`s²/n` doubles, `t` drops to about 1.75, and `df` falls to 8 with a critical value near 2.31.
The same 5-point gap would not be significant.

> Hypotheses, t, degrees of freedom, critical value, decision. The same five steps decide every
> t-test, and the result depends as much on sample size and spread as on the gap itself.
