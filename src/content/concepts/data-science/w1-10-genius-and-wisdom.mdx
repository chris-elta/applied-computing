The slides split two jobs. **Software developers are hired to produce code.** **Data
scientists are hired to produce insights.** The difference in output decides which
quality counts.

> Genius shows in finding the right answer.
> Wisdom shows in avoiding the wrong answers.

Data science, like most things, benefits more from wisdom than from genius.

## Why avoiding wrong answers comes first

Insights are claims about the world, and the world will not tell you when you are mistaken.
A bug in code usually crashes something. A flaw in an analysis — a mislabelled column, a
skewed sample, a pattern that is really an artefact — produces a clean-looking chart that
is simply wrong.

Since you cannot always check against the truth, the protection is the habit of doubting your
own result before someone else does.

## Where wisdom comes from

The next slide lists four sources:

1. **Experience.**
2. **General knowledge.**
3. **Listening to others.**
4. **Humility** — observing how often you have been wrong, and why and how.

The fourth is the one to notice. It is not enough to be wrong; you have to *record* it and
work out what led you there.

The lecturer states the aim of the course in these terms: to pass on wisdom, drawn from
experience of how difficult it is to make good predictions.

> You cannot check every answer against the truth, so you build the habit of asking how you
> might be wrong.
