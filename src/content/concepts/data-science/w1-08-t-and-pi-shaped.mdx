The slides on skills for data science use a picture. Draw a T: the horizontal bar is breadth,
the vertical stem is depth.

## T-shaped

A T-shaped person is a **broad-range generalist with deep expertise** in one area. The
lecture's example:

- **Breadth:** basic skills of software engineering, business, distributed computing and
  communication.
- **Depth:** deep skills in deep neural networks (a *technique*) and medical systems (a
  *domain*).

## Why this shape

Look back at the case study. Building the music product needed modelling, operations, safety,
business and more. Nobody can be expert in all of it, but everybody on the team must know
enough of each area to talk to the people who are.

That is what the top bar is for. It lets a specialist understand what an engineer or a lawyer
is worried about without becoming one. The stem is what you bring that others cannot.

## The latest buzzword: π

The final slide extends the metaphor to **π-shaped** people: the same breadth, with **two**
deep areas. In the lecture's own example, technique and domain are the natural pair — knowing
how to build the model and knowing the field it is used in.

The lecturer calls this "the latest buzzword", which is a hint not to treat the letters as
a rule. The useful idea is the one underneath: breadth so you can collaborate, and depth so
you contribute.

> Depth makes you useful. Breadth makes you useful to a team.
