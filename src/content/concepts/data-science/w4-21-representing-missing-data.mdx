A table cell can be empty for different reasons, and the lecture asks you to represent them
properly. Three of its examples:

- **What is the year of death of a living person?** There is no such year yet.
- **What about a field left blank or filled with an obviously outlandish value?** Someone
  typed 9999, or −1, or "N/A", to mean "I don't know".
- **What is the frequency of events too rare to see?** Nothing was observed, but that does
  not mean it never happens.

The slide's verdict: **setting such values to zero is generally wrong.**

## Why zero is the trap

A zero is a measurement. It says "we looked and the answer was nothing". A blank says "we
don't know". Mixing them in a table means every later mean, correlation and z-score treats
unknowns as real data.

A placeholder like 9999 is worse, because it looks like a legitimate value and can become the
largest number in the data set. That is how an outlier gets created by a data entry habit.

## What to do instead

Use the data type's real marker for "no value" (`NaN`, `NULL` or `None`, depending on the
tool), find out why each blank exists, and decide deliberately what to do with it. The next
concept covers one of those choices.

> Missing is not zero. Keep the difference visible in the data, and decide what to do about
> it where you can see it.
