"Garbage in, garbage out" is the heading of the lecture's cleaning section. It lists five
problems that make field data hard to analyse sensibly:

1. distinguishing errors from artifacts,
2. data compatibility and unification,
3. imputing missing values,
4. estimating unobserved (zero) counts,
5. outlier detection.

The first is the one that organizes the rest.

## Two kinds of wrong

| | What it is | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Error** | Information that is fundamentally lost | Acquisition — the measurement or record was never right |
| **Artifact** | A systematic problem | Processing done to the data |

The difference matters because they call for different responses. Lost information stays
lost; the best you can do is estimate or flag it. An artifact can be removed, but only if you
notice it.

## The sniff test

Artifacts do not announce themselves. The code runs, the table has rows and the plot draws.
The lecture's remedy is the **sniff test**: examine the product closely enough to get a whiff
of something bad.

That depends on knowing roughly what a correct answer looks like, which is why the next
concept starts with a question about the *shape* of a result before showing it.

> Before you trust an output, ask what it should look like. A surprise is either a discovery
> or an artifact, and artifacts are far more common.
