By this point you have a toolbox: weighted scores, Elo, Borda, graph orderings, PageRank.
Each had some weakness. It is reasonable to ask whether a better method exists that has
none. The slide's answer is a theorem: **there is no ranking system that satisfies all
desirable properties.**

## The properties

What would you ask of a fair way to combine many voters' rankings into one? The slide lists
five requirements, and each sounds too obvious to dispute.

| property | in words |
| --- | --- |
| **Complete** | Given A and B, it must pick one or say equal preference |
| **Transitive** | If A > B and B > C, then A > C |
| **Unanimity** | If every voter prefers A to B, then A wins over B |
| **No dictator** | The outcome cannot depend only on one voter's preferences |
| **Independence** | The preference of A to B should not depend on preferences for other candidates |

Arrow's theorem says no system has all five at once. This is not a matter of nobody having
found one yet. It is proved that none can exist.

## The slide's three voters

| voter | Red | Green | Blue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| X | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| Y | 2 | 3 | 1 |
| Z | 3 | 1 | 2 |

Each cell is the position that voter gave that colour. So X prefers Red, then Green, then
Blue. Now hold three two-way contests by majority.

- **Red vs Green.** X and Y put Red ahead. Red wins 2 to 1.
- **Green vs Blue.** X and Z put Green ahead. Green wins 2 to 1.
- **Blue vs Red.** Y and Z put Blue ahead. Blue wins 2 to 1.

**Red beats Green, Green beats Blue, Blue beats Red.** That is a cycle. Each voter was
perfectly consistent, and the group is not. Transitivity fails although nobody did anything
unreasonable.

This also explains the cycles in the graph-ordering concept. They need not be upsets or
noise. A group of consistent individuals can produce one.

## What breaks if you force an answer

Try to escape and you give up some other property.

- Declare Red the winner because X said so, and X is a dictator.
- Use Borda. Here every colour gets `1 + 2 + 3 = 6`, a tie. In general a Borda result can
  change when a candidate is added or removed, which breaks independence.
- Refuse to rank them, and the system is not complete.

Every way out costs one of the five.

## What to take from it

The theorem does not say ranking is pointless. Search engines, chess ratings and elections
work well enough to run the world on. It says that every ranking method contains a
compromise, so "which method is correct?" is the wrong question. The useful question is
which property you can best afford to lose for the problem in front of you.

This matches the start of the week. Scores had no gold standard to be checked against, and
rankings have no perfect method to be built with. In both cases you make a choice, say what
it was, and test it on cases you understand.

> No ranking method is beyond criticism. Choose the compromise that suits the problem, and
> say which one you chose.
