You have a mean and a standard deviation and nothing else. Can you say anything firm about
where the data lies? Yes, and you can say it without knowing the shape.

## The inequality

The slide states it in words. **Regardless of how data is distributed, at least `1 − 1/k²`
of the mass must lie within `k` sigma of the mean.**

```
P( μ − kσ < X < μ + kσ )  ≥  1 − 1/k²
```

Plug in values of `k`:

| `k` (sigma from the mean) | Guaranteed within the interval |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 0% (no information) |
| 2 | 75% |
| 3 | about 89% |
| 5 | 96% |

The slide draws out the case `k = 2`: **at least 75% lies within two sigma of the mean**, so
at most 25% is more than two sigma away.

## Worked examples

**Response times.** Response times have mean 100 ms and standard deviation 10 ms. Without
knowing anything about the shape, at least 75% of responses fall between 80 and 120 ms, and
at least 89% between 70 and 130 ms. That is enough to set a service target or spot an
unusual reading.

**Example 1: exam scores.** Scores have mean 70 and standard deviation 8, shape unknown.
What is the most that can be said about the share outside 50 to 90?

- 50 to 90 is `70 ± 20`, so `kσ = 20` and `k = 20/8 = 2.5`.
- `P(|X − 70| ≥ 20) ≤ 1/2.5² = 1/6.25 = 0.16`

At most 16% of students score below 50 or above 90, so at least 84% score between 50 and 90.

**Example 2: delivery times.** A courier's delivery time has mean 30 min and variance 25, so
`σ = 5`. Find a lower bound on the probability that a delivery takes between 20 and 40
minutes.

- 20 to 40 is `30 ± 10`, so `k = 10/5 = 2`.
- `P(|X − 30| ≥ 10) ≤ 1/2² = 0.25`
- Taking the complement, `P(20 < X < 40) ≥ 1 − 0.25 = 0.75`

At least 75% of deliveries arrive within 10 minutes of the mean. Note the first step: when
you are given a variance, take its square root to get `σ` before finding `k`.

## Why it is loose, and why that is fine

The slide adds that **even tighter bounds apply for normal distributions**. For the bell
curve, about 95% lies within two sigma, against Chebyshev's 75%.

The inequality is weak because it has to cover every distribution, including awkward ones.
Its value is the guarantee: no distribution with a given mean and spread can break it. So it
is a safe fallback when you do not know the shape.

## The misconception

Some people quote the normal distribution's 95% for any data. That is wrong unless the data
is close to normal. When you cannot vouch for the shape, use Chebyshev.

This finishes the idea from the previous concept: the mean and standard deviation do not
just describe a distribution loosely, they *constrain* it.

> Whatever the shape, most of the data sits within a few standard deviations of the mean.
> Two numbers give a guarantee.
