The last route is the least glamorous. The lecture's framing: *sometimes you must work for
your data instead of stealing it.*

Much historical data still exists only on paper or in PDFs. Getting it into a table needs
manual entry and curation, and no scraper can do that for you.

## Doing the sum

Before deciding it is too much, estimate it. At one record per minute, 1,000 records take
1,000 minutes, a little under 17 hours — **about two working days**. A hundred thousand would
be well over a year of full-time effort, so at some size you need a crowd instead, which is
the previous concept.

The slide's conclusion is: *often projects require sweat equity.* Finding the right data set
is the critical issue, and sometimes the right data set does not exist until someone makes
it.

> Estimate the cost of doing it by hand before ruling it out. A small, correct data set you
> built yourself can beat a large one you cannot use.
