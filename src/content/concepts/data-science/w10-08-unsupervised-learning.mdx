In supervised learning the data comes with answers. In **unsupervised learning** it does not.

## The picture

Slides 8 and 9 draw the same kind of data twice, with two features on the axes.

- **Supervised** (slide 8): the points are marked as two kinds, crosses and circles. The task
  is to learn a boundary between them, so a new point can be labelled.
- **Unsupervised** (slide 9): the points are all the same. No one has said what kind each is.
  The task is to find structure on your own, such as two clusters of points that sit close
  together.

The course notes put it this way: unsupervised learning allows us to approach problems with
little or no idea what our results should look like. We can derive structure from data where we
don't necessarily know the effect of the variables. And "there is no feedback based on the
prediction results": no teacher to correct you.

## Clustering

The commonest unsupervised task is **clustering**: grouping data by the relationships among its
variables. Slide 10's applications:

| application | what is clustered |
| --- | --- |
| organising computing clusters | which machines work together, so they can be placed near each other |
| social network analysis | groups of people who interact |
| market segmentation | customers with similar behaviour, to target separately |

The notes add an example of their own: take 1,000 essays on the US economy and automatically
group them by word frequency, sentence length, page count and so on.

## Not only clustering

The notes also give a non-clustering example: the **cocktail party problem**. From a recording
of a room full of voices and music, separate the individual sources. There are no labels saying
which sound is whose. The algorithm finds the structure, independent sources mixed together,
from the data alone.

## Why it is harder to evaluate

Week 9's metrics all compare predictions with correct answers. Unsupervised learning has none,
so there is no accuracy to compute. Whether the clusters are *good* is judged by whether they
are useful, which often needs a person to look at them. The rest of week 10 is supervised: linear
regression.

> Unsupervised learning finds structure in data without labels: clusters of similar items, or
> sources mixed together. With no right answers, there is no direct accuracy to measure.
