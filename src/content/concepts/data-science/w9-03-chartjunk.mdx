**Chartjunk** is the slide's name for "extraneous visual elements [that] distract from the
message the data is trying to tell". It is the data-ink ratio seen from the other side: the
ink you should remove.

## Three kinds

| kind | example | why it hurts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| extra dimensionality | 3-D bars, perspective pies | depth distorts sizes and hides values behind each other |
| uninformative colouring | every bar a different colour for no reason | colour suggests a category that does not exist |
| excessive grids and decoration | heavy gridlines, clip art, textured backgrounds | competes with the data for attention |

> In an exciting graphic, the data tells the story, not the chartjunk.

## A simplification in five steps

Slides 9–13 take one bar chart of monthly percentages and keep asking *can you simplify
further?*

1. **Start:** dark bars on a pale-blue shaded panel with a full grid.
2. **Drop the shading.** The colour behind the bars carried nothing.
3. **Drop the vertical gridlines.** The bars already separate the months.
4. **Thin the horizontal gridlines and remove the frame.** A reader still needs to judge
   heights, so a faint reference remains.
5. **"Less is more":** bars, axis labels, nothing else.

At each step the question is the same one from the data-ink concept: *would the reader lose
anything if this went?* The data itself is never touched. Slide 14 makes the practical point
that Matplotlib can produce these clean plots; the defaults are a starting point, not the
finished chart.

## Where the limit is

Simplifying does not mean removing context. Axis labels, units, a title that says what the
chart shows, and a legend when colour does encode something are all information. Later in
the week, slide 41 shows an election graphic that uses colour and a winding shape to tell a
story in one view: decoration that carries meaning is not junk.

> Chartjunk is anything that draws the eye without telling it something. Remove it in steps,
> asking each time what the reader would lose, and stop before you remove the labels.
