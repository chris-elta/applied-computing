`J` is a function of two parameters, which is hard to picture. Slide 20 simplifies: fix the
intercept at zero, so the hypothesis is a line through the origin.

```
hθ(x) = θ₁x            J(θ₁) = (1 / 2m) Σᵢ₌₁ᵐ (θ₁x⁽ⁱ⁾ − y⁽ⁱ⁾)²
```

Now there is one dial, `θ₁`, the slope, and `J` is an ordinary function of one variable you can
draw.

## Two pictures side by side

Slides 21–24 put two plots next to each other:

| left | right |
| --- | --- |
| `hθ(x)`, **a function of x**, for one fixed `θ₁` | `J(θ₁)`, **a function of the parameter** `θ₁` |
| the data and one line | one point per line you could have drawn |

The training data is three points: (1, 1), (2, 2) and (3, 3), with `m = 3`.

## Computing points on J

**θ₁ = 1.** The line `y = x` passes through all three points. Every error is 0, so `J(1) = 0`.

**θ₁ = 0.5.** The line is too shallow:

| x | y | 0.5x | error | squared |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | 0.5 | −0.5 | 0.25 |
| 2 | 2 | 1 | −1 | 1 |
| 3 | 3 | 1.5 | −1.5 | 2.25 |

`J(0.5) = 3.5 / 6 ≈ 0.58`.

**θ₁ = 0.** The line is flat on the x-axis. The squared errors are 1, 4 and 9, so
`J(0) = 14 / 6 ≈ 2.33`.

Plot those, plus a few more (`θ₁ = 1.5` gives the same 0.58 as 0.5 by symmetry, `θ₁ = −0.5`
gives about 5.25), and the right-hand plot becomes a **bowl**: a parabola with its lowest point
at `θ₁ = 1`.

## What the picture says

Each point on the right-hand curve *is* a whole line on the left. Moving along the curve is
trying different slopes. The lowest point is the best line.

So "learning the parameters" has become "find the bottom of the bowl". With one parameter you
could do it by eye; with two the bowl becomes a surface (next concept); with hundreds you need an
algorithm that walks downhill, which is gradient descent.

That `J` is a single smooth bowl here is not luck. A squared error of a line is a quadratic in
`θ₁`, and quadratics with a positive leading term have exactly one minimum. That property
returns in the gradient-descent concepts, where it means there are no false bottoms to get
stuck in.

> Fix θ₀ = 0 and J becomes a function of the slope alone: a bowl, whose lowest point is the
> slope that fits best. Every point on J is one line through the data.
