Gradient descent walks to the minimum of `J`. The **normal equation** jumps there: "a method to
solve for θ analytically" (slide 31).

## The idea, in one dimension

Slide 33 starts with θ a single number and `J(θ) = aθ² + bθ + c`, a parabola. At its lowest point
the slope is flat, so you solve

```
dJ/dθ = 0
```

for θ. No iterations, no α.

With many parameters, `J(θ₀, …, θₙ)` is a bowl in n + 1 dimensions. Its minimum is where every
partial derivative is zero at once:

```
∂J/∂θⱼ = 0        for every j
```

and you solve that system for θ₀, θ₁, …, θₙ.

## The result

Solving it for linear regression gives a closed form (slide 35):

```
θ = (XᵀX)⁻¹ Xᵀ y
```

The course notes say the proof is involved and you do not need the details. Slide 32 states the
same result in other letters, for a system `Aw = b`, and adds:

> Thus least squares optimisation reduces to inversion and multiplying matrices.

## Building X and y

Slide 35 uses the four-house table with `m = 4` and adds the `x₀ = 1` column:

```
     ⎡ 1  2104  5  1  45 ⎤          ⎡ 460 ⎤
X =  ⎢ 1  1416  3  2  40 ⎥     y =  ⎢ 232 ⎥
     ⎢ 1  1534  3  2  30 ⎥          ⎢ 315 ⎥
     ⎣ 1   852  2  1  36 ⎦          ⎣ 178 ⎦
```

`X` is `m × (n + 1)`, here 4 × 5, and `y` is `m × 1`. Check the dimensions of the formula, as slide
32 does:

| piece | size |
| --- | --- |
| `XᵀX` | `(n+1) × m` times `m × (n+1)` = `(n+1) × (n+1)` |
| `(XᵀX)⁻¹` | `(n+1) × (n+1)`: square, so it can have an inverse |
| `Xᵀy` | `(n+1) × m` times `m × 1` = `(n+1) × 1` |
| `θ` | `(n+1) × (n+1)` times `(n+1) × 1` = `(n+1) × 1` |

One parameter per column of `X`, as it should be.

## A worked example

Two points, (1, 2) and (2, 4):

```
X = ⎡ 1  1 ⎤     y = ⎡ 2 ⎤     XᵀX = ⎡ 2  3 ⎤     Xᵀy = ⎡  6 ⎤
    ⎣ 1  2 ⎦         ⎣ 4 ⎦           ⎣ 3  5 ⎦           ⎣ 10 ⎦

(XᵀX)⁻¹ = ⎡  5  −3 ⎤        θ = ⎡  5·6 − 3·10 ⎤ = ⎡ 0 ⎤
          ⎣ −3   2 ⎦            ⎣ −3·6 + 2·10 ⎦   ⎣ 2 ⎦
```

`hθ(x) = 0 + 2x`, exactly through both points. In code, `np.linalg.pinv(X.T @ X) @ X.T @ y`.

## No feature scaling needed

The notes point out that the normal equation **does not need feature scaling**. Scaling helped
gradient descent take a straight path down a round bowl. The normal equation takes no path; it
computes the answer directly, whatever the units.

> Setting every partial derivative of J to zero and solving gives θ = (XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀy: the best
> parameters in one step, with no α, no iterations and no scaling.
