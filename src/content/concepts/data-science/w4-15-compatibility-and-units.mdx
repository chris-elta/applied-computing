Data from different places has to be **massaged to make "apple to apple" comparisons**. The
slide lists five areas, which the next concepts take in turn:

- unit conversions,
- number and character code representations,
- name unification,
- time and date unification,
- financial unification.

## Units

The lecture's example is expensive: NASA's **Mars Climate Orbiter exploded in 1999 due to a
metric-to-English conversion issue.** One system produced numbers in one set of units and
another consumed them as if they were in a different set. Neither number was wrong; they were
in different units.

Three notes from the slide:

- **Even sticking to metric has inconsistencies:** centimetres, metres and kilometres.
- **Bimodal distributions can indicate trouble.** Two sources in different units show up as
  two humps.
- **Z-scores are dimensionless quantities.** Normalizing, which the next concept explains,
  removes units from the comparison.

The habit that catches these is the one from the artifacts concepts: look at the distribution
of every variable after merging, and ask whether it has the shape you would expect.

> Vigilance in data integration is essential. A column called "height" does not say what the
> numbers are measured in.
