Bars, pies and stacks all show categorical data: a count or proportion for each category.
They differ in how easy they make the comparison.

## Bar plots against pie charts

From slide 25: bar plots "show the frequency or proportion of categorical variables". Pie
charts "use more space and are harder to read and compare".

The reason is perception. Bars put every value on one shared baseline, so comparing two is
comparing two lengths. A pie encodes values as angles and areas, which people judge poorly,
and two similar slices on opposite sides of the circle are nearly impossible to rank.

The lecture is not absolutist. Pie charts "are arguably better for showing percentages of
totality, and people do seem to like them, so they may be harmless in small amounts": a few
slices, adding to a whole.

Slide 26 asks *which pie chart is better?* The exploded 3-D pie distorts every slice: the
ones at the front gain area from perspective. If you use a pie, keep it flat.

## Stacked bars

Partition each bar into pieces and you get a **stacked bar chart**, which shows a total and
its parts at once. Slide 31 draws Titanic survival by class twice:

| version | shows well | hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| counts | how many passengers each class had, and how many died | the *rate* of survival per class |
| proportions (each bar to 100%) | survival rate per class: far higher in first | the size of each class |

Pick the version that answers your question; often you need both.

## Stacked area against lines

Slide 30 warns that in a stacked area chart it is "hard to see trends in middle areas of
stack". Only the bottom band sits on a flat baseline. Every band above it rides on the ones
below, so its edges move even when its own value does not. Redrawn as separate lines on one
axis, each series has its own trend again.

The same problem applies to the upper segments of a stacked bar, which is why stacking works
best with few pieces, or when the bottom segment is the one that matters.

> Compare categories with bars on a shared baseline. Use pies only for a few parts of one
> whole, and flat. Stack only when the total matters, knowing that every layer above the
> first is hard to read.
