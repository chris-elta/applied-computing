A dataset can have thousands of values. Nobody reads them all. **Descriptive statistics**
provides ways to capture the properties of a given dataset or sample in a few numbers.

The lecture sorts these into two families.

## Central tendency

**Central tendency** measures describe the centre around which the data is distributed:
where is a typical value? The next two concepts cover the mean, median and geometric mean.

## Variation

**Variation**, or variability, measures describe the spread: how far the measurements lie
from the centre. Standard deviation is the main one.

## Why both

One number is never enough. Consider how the same average can arise:

| Dataset | Values | Mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 48, 49, 50, 51, 52 | 50 |
| B | 0, 25, 50, 75, 100 | 50 |

A tells you what to expect. B tells you to expect almost anything. If you report only the
mean, you have said the same thing about both.

So a description of data has two jobs: say where it is, and say how much it varies. The
rest of the week works through each in turn, then shows how much a mean and spread together
can guarantee.

> Centre says where. Spread says how sure you can be.
