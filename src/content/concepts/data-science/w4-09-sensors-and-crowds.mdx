Two sources the lecture treats as ways to build data rather than find it.

## Sensor data

The "Internet of Things" can do surprising things with data it was not built to collect:

- images and video, such as measuring weather from Flickr photos;
- accelerometers in cell phones used to measure earthquakes;
- GPS on taxis used to identify traffic flows.

The lecture's advice is to **build logging systems: storage is cheap.** Recording what a
system does costs little now, and you cannot go back for data you did not keep. The question
you want answered next year may not be the one you had today.

## Crowdsourcing

Many open data resources were built by teams of contributors. Wikipedia and WikiData are the
slide's examples, along with IMDB.

You can also **buy** a crowd. Platforms like **Amazon Turk** let you pay armies of people to
help gather data, such as **human annotation** — people labelling items that a program cannot
yet label.

The two ideas are alike in one respect: neither data set was collected to answer *your*
question, and each reflects whoever happened to contribute or be sensed. The bias concepts
later this week return to that.

> Log everything you can afford to. And when you need labels, a crowd can be a data source
> you pay for.
