Facebook, Google, Amazon and Blue Cross hold exactly the data a data scientist would like:
users, transactions, logs. Most organizations have, or should have, internal data sets that
matter to their own business. That is the **proprietary** route.

The lecture is plain about the limit: **getting outside access is usually impossible.** What
companies do sometimes release is a rate-limited API, as Twitter and Google have. You get a
trickle through a narrow door.

If you work for the organization, the position is reversed: internal data is the best place to
start, and the lecture suggests every organization should be collecting it.

## Real public data is decreasing

There are two pressures on open data, both from large language models.

- **Providers tighten up.** The commercial promise of LLMs has made once-open sources, such
  as Twitter and Reddit, restrict access to their text.
- **The web fills with generated content.** AI-generated text and images online make it
  harder to train *improved* models, since new data is increasingly produced by earlier
  models rather than by people.

The first makes data harder to get. The second makes the data you can get less trustworthy
as a record of the world.

> Assume the richest data belongs to someone who will not share it, and plan your project
> around what you can legitimately obtain.
