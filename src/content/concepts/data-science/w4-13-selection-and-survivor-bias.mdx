The slide says **data bias occurs when data is inaccurate, incomplete or missing, which can
lead to an inaccurate representation of a population.** And: **the best way to clean data is
to eliminate systematic biases in data collection.** It is cheaper to avoid the problem at
collection than to repair it afterwards.

The lecture's diagram sorts biases by where they enter — in **gathering** data, in
**analysing** it, and in **applying** the result. This concept covers the two that happen at
gathering time.

## Selection bias

**Selection bias** is over- or under-sampling certain groups. If a phone poll only reaches
people who answer calls from unknown numbers, the sample is those people, whatever the
population is.

## Survivor bias

**Survivor bias** arises when you select at the end: *by selecting at the end, you
undersample who exits early.*

The slide's picture is a bomber covered in marks where returning planes were hit. The tempting
reading is to armor the marked areas. But these are the planes that came back. The planes
hit in the unmarked areas, the engines, did not return to be counted. The gaps are the
information.

| Observation | Tempting reading | Correct reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Few holes in the engine area | Engines rarely get hit | Hits there are fatal |
| Successful companies share a trait | The trait causes success | Failed companies with it are not in your list |

> Ask who is *not* in your data and why. The missing records often say more than the
> present ones.
