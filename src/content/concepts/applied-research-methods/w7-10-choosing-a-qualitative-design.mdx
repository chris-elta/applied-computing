Seven designs is a lot to keep apart, and several look similar from a distance. The
quickest way to tell them apart is to ask what each one studies, and what kind of question it
answers. Everything below comes from the preceding seven concepts.

## The seven at a glance

| Design | Studies | Typical question | Main data | Produces |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Case study** | One or a few individuals, programs or events, in context | What happened here, and why, given the setting? | Several kinds: observation, interviews, documents, records | Patterns connected to the larger picture |
| **Ethnography** | A whole group with a shared culture | What are this group's norms, beliefs and practices? | Field notes and reflective journals from long fieldwork | A personal, literary account of the culture |
| **Phenomenological study** | A shared experience | What is it like to experience this? | Long interviews with a small, selected sample | A description of the lived experience |
| **Grounded theory** | Actions and interactions, where little is understood | What explains how this process works? | Interviews, chosen by constant comparison | A theory built entirely from the data |
| **Narrative inquiry** | Personal experience, through stories | How did this person's experience unfold? | Recollections and stories | A rich narrative of setting and sequence |
| **Content analysis** | A body of communication | How often do these patterns or themes appear? | Documents and media, coded into categories | Frequency tabulations |
| **Discourse analysis** | Language-based communication in context | What is this language doing, for these people? | Conversation and text, read with their context | An interpretation of constructed meaning |

## Four pairs that get confused

| If you are between… | Ask |
| --- | --- |
| Case study and ethnography | Is the focus a **case** (an event or program) or a group's **culture**? |
| Phenomenology and narrative inquiry | Is the experience **shared** by several people, or a **personal** story? |
| Content and discourse analysis | Are you **counting categories**, or **interpreting meaning in context**? |
| Grounded theory and everything else | Do you want to **build a theory**, starting with none? |

## Working back from the question

Write your research question first, then check which column it belongs in. "What is it like
to…" points to phenomenology. "How often does… appear in…" points to content analysis. "How
does this group…" points to ethnography. If no row fits, the question may be quantitative,
or not yet specific enough. Week 6's FINER criteria apply either way.

Remember also what all seven share, from *What Makes Research Qualitative*: none of them can
establish cause and effect, and in each the researcher is the instrument.

> Pick the design from the question, not the other way round. Each of the seven answers a
> different kind of question.
