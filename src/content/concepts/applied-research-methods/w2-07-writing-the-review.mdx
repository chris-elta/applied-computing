The last step is the writing. The lecture's advice comes in two groups.

## Structure and honesty

- **Plan the overall organisational structure** of the review before you write.
- **Emphasise relatedness**: every section should show how the literature relates to *your
  problem*, not simply what exists.
- **Give credit where credit is due**, with appropriate citations.
- **Review the literature; do not reproduce it.** Know the difference between *describing*
  the literature and *plagiarising* it.
- **Minimise direct quotations** from other people's writing.

The second-to-last item is where students go wrong. The test of whether you have described
rather than plagiarised: could you explain the argument without looking at the paper, and
have you said whose argument it is?

## Readability

- Provide **transitional phrases, sentences or paragraphs** so readers can follow your train
  of thought. A reader should never wonder why you moved from one source to the next.
- **Summarise** what you have said (the same habit as for any section, from Week 1).
- Remember that **your first draft will not be your last**.
- **Ask others for advice and feedback.**

Put the three weeks together and the flow is: state the problem (Week 1), find the literature
and read it critically (Week 2), then use what you learned to design the study (Week 3).

> A literature review is an argument about what is known. Every paragraph should be doing
> something for your problem.
