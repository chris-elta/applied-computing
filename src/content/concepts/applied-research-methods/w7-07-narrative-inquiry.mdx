Ask a senior engineer how they ended up specialising in security and you will get a story.
It might involve a mentor, a breach they cleaned up in their second job, and a company that
let them move teams. A **narrative inquiry** treats stories like that as data.

## What it studies

A method for studying **complex, multifaceted phenomena** that relies on **people's
recollections and stories** about their experiences of them:

- **past and present** interactions with others, within the context;
- **personal** rather than **shared** experience, unlike phenomenology.

The second point marks the line between two designs that look alike:

| | Phenomenological study | Narrative inquiry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asks | What is it like to experience this? | How did this person's experience unfold? |
| Experience | **Shared** by the participants | **Personal** to each storyteller |
| Typical output | A description of the experience in common | A **rich narrative** |

## Three dimensions

The lecture places each story in a **three-dimensional narrative space**:

| Dimension | Asks of the story | In the security example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Interaction** | Who else was involved, and how? | The mentor; the team that cleaned up the breach |
| **Continuity** | How does the past lead to the present? | Second job, then the breach, then the move to the security team |
| **Situation** | Where, and in what context? | A small company with no security staff, so someone had to step up |

Reading for all three turns an anecdote into evidence. A story that names only the
breach is a memory. The same story read for who was involved, what came before and after,
and where it happened explains how a career took its shape.

## The result

The output is the **researcher's rich narrative**. It gives a better understanding of the
**setting, context, time period and sequence** of the phenomenon. The sequence is what
narrative inquiry contributes that other designs do not: it tells you the order in which
things happened, from the inside.

Stories are recollections, so they are not neutral records. *Three Biases*, later this
week, calls this **response bias**: participants may give inaccurate descriptions. The
storyteller's interpretation is part of what is studied, but it should not be mistaken for a
log of events.

> Narrative inquiry studies one person's experience as a story, reading it for who was
> involved, how it unfolded over time, and where it happened.
