There is always something new to learn about a topic, so a literature search cannot end
because you ran out of literature. The lecture instead gives signs that the *preliminary*
review has done its job. It is also worded carefully: you should **continue to look for new
research reports** as your project runs.

## The signs

You have probably searched enough when:

- you see **repetitive patterns** in the material you are reading;
- the **arguments, methodologies and findings become familiar**;
- the **same key researchers and studies** are cited over and over;
- you are **no longer encountering new viewpoints**.

All four are the same thing seen from different sides: new papers are no longer telling you
anything you did not already know.

## Two cautions

First, repetition is evidence about *the area you have been searching*. If every paper came
from one database or one set of keywords, the repetition may only mean you have explored one
corner. A quick search with different keywords tests that.

Second, this is a stopping rule for the *preliminary* review. The lecture is clear that you
should continue to look for new research reports. A new paper that overturns your framing,
found late, is better than one found after you have published.

> Stop when the literature stops surprising you, then keep one eye open.
