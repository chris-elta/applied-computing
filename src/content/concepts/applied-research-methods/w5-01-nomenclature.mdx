Week 4 introduced the idea; Week 5 uses it every line, so here is the vocabulary pinned down.

- A **population** includes each element from the set of observations that can be made.
- A **sample** consists only of observations drawn from the population.
- A **parameter** is a measurable characteristic of the population, such as its mean or
  standard deviation.
- A **statistic** is a measurable characteristic of a sample.

## Greek for the population, Roman for the sample

A convention keeps them apart on the page:

| Quantity | Population (parameter) | Sample (statistic) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mean | `μ` | `x̄` |
| Standard deviation | `σ` | `s` |
| Number of observations | `N` | `n` |

A formula that mixes them is therefore telling you something. In `z = (x̄ − μ) / (σ / √n)`, the
numerator compares what you saw (`x̄`) with what a claim says (`μ`).

## The other measures

Slide 5 restates the summary measures from Week 4:

| Measure | In one line |
| --- | --- |
| **Mean** | The average |
| **Median** | The middle value, smallest to largest |
| **Mode** | The most common value |
| **Variance** | How data points differ from the mean |
| **Standard deviation** | The square root of the variance |
| **Confidence interval** | Later this week |

The deck also lists the **percentile** and **standard score**, which the next concept defines.

> Parameters are what you want to know. Statistics are what you have. Inference connects them.
