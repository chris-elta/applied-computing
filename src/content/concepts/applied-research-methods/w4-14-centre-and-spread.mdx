Given a pile of numbers, three questions come up. The lecture states them and gives measures
for each. The first two are covered here; correlation, the third, is taken up in Week 5.

1. Where is the **centre**? (Central tendency)
2. How **spread out** is it? (Variability)
3. Do two variables move together? (Correlation)

## Central tendency

| Measure | Definition | Weak spot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mode** | The number that occurs most often | May not be unique, and ignores everything else |
| **Median** | The numerical centre of the data | Ignores how far the other values are |
| **Mean** | The arithmetic average | **Pulled by extreme values** |
| **Geometric mean** | A centre based on geometric progression, such as growth | Only for multiplicative quantities |

For `1, 2, 2, 3, 12`: mode 2, median 2, mean 4. The mean is dragged above four of the five
values by the 12. That is the reason to report more than one centre.

## Variability

Variability is how far values **cluster around the central tendency**.

| Measure | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Range** | Highest value minus lowest |
| **Average deviation** | The average of the differences between each value and the mean |
| **Standard deviation** | The distance of values from the mean, in a form that **takes negative numbers into account** |
| **Variance** | The standard deviation squared |
| **Norm-referenced score** | Where each person stands relative to others in the group |
| **Standard score** | How far a value is from the mean, in standard deviation units |

A note on the second and third rows. Raw differences from the mean cancel to zero, so
*something* has to handle the minus signs. The average deviation does it by ignoring signs;
the standard deviation squares the differences before averaging, then takes a square root to
return to the original units.

> A mean without a spread is half a description. Two samples with the same mean can behave
> completely differently.
