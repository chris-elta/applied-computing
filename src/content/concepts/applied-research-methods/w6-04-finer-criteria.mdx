FINER is a checklist for whether a research question is worth pursuing. It comes from
clinical research (Hulley and colleagues), and the lecture reads each letter for a
computer-science Master's project.

| | Criterion | What it means for a CS Master's project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F** | **Feasible** | Data you can get, compute you have access to, skills you hold or can acquire **in weeks**, time left in the program. |
| **I** | **Interesting** | To **you**, because you will live with it for a while; and to a **community**, because a venue publishes this kind of result. |
| **N** | **Novel** | Not already answered. Check arXiv, DBLP, Google Scholar. Do your literature review. |
| **E** | **Ethical** | Consent for data, dual-use potential, human-subjects approval where needed, environmental cost of compute. |
| **R** | **Relevant** | Someone would change what they do based on the answer. **Name that person or role.** |

## Reading the table closely

**Feasible** is concrete on purpose. It is not "could this be done?" but "can *you* do it,
with *this* data and compute, in the time *left*?" A skill you would need a year to learn
fails. One you can pick up in a few weeks passes.

**Interesting** has two audiences. Your own interest matters for a practical reason: a
question you do not care about is hard to sustain through months of setbacks. The community's
interest is checked by asking whether some venue publishes results like this.

**Novel** is where the literature review from Week 2 earns its keep. You cannot claim
something is unanswered without having looked.

**Ethical** goes beyond human subjects. **Dual use** means the result could be used for
harm as easily as for good. The **environmental cost of compute** counts too, which matters
for anyone planning to train large models.

**Relevant** carries the instruction most often skipped: name the person. This is the same
demand Heilmeier's "Who cares?" makes in the next concept, and the answer to the reviewer's
*"Why would anyone care about this result?"*

## Using it

FINER is a filter to run on candidate questions, not a template to fill in afterwards. A
question that fails F or E should be reshaped before it is written up, not defended. Week 1's
habit of thinking through feasibility when stating the problem is the same idea in a smaller
form.

> Feasible for you, interesting to someone, not yet answered, defensible to do, and useful to
> a person you can name.
