The best-known qualitative design is also the most intuitive. Pick one thing and study it
thoroughly: one team, one product launch, one outage.

## What a case study is

The researcher looks at **one or a few** individuals, programs or events:

- **in depth**, over an **extended period**;
- using **several different kinds of data**, such as observations, interviews,
  technology-based communication (email, chat, tickets), documents, records and audiovisual
  materials;
- including a **portrayal of the context** around the case.

The context is part of the design, not background. The investigation may take into account
the broader **physical, historical, cultural, social and economic** contexts, and consider
how they affect the case. A study of one team's agile adoption that ignores a hiring freeze
running at the same time has missed part of its own subject.

## Analysis

The lecture gives five steps:

| Step | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| **Organise** details about the case | Put events in order and collect the facts in one place |
| **Categorise** the data | Group material into meaningful categories |
| **Interpret single instances** | Ask what a particular document, incident or remark means |
| **Identify patterns** | Look across instances for themes that recur |
| **Synthesise and generalise** | Draw the patterns into conclusions |

The movement is from the detail of single instances up to patterns, then to broader
statements.

## The report

A case-study report gives:

- the **rationale** for studying this case;
- a **detailed description** of the related facts;
- a **description of the data** collected;
- a **discussion of the patterns** found;
- the **connection to the larger scheme of things**.

The last item answers the obvious objection, *it is only one case*. You cannot claim that
every company would migrate the same way. You can show how this case relates to what is
already known, and what it suggests others should look for.

Sampling for case studies, including choosing an extreme case on purpose, comes later this
week in *Purposive Sampling*.

> A case study gets its value from depth and context. It looks closely at one thing, in its
> setting, using every kind of evidence available.
