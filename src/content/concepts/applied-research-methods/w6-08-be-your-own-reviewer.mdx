The lecture ends where it began, with reviewers. The second slide listed three comments
reviewers write over and over. The last one gives six, each paired with the fix you can apply
*before* submitting.

| What a reviewer might say | The fix |
| --- | --- |
| *"The contribution is unclear."* | A **one-sentence claim** in the abstract, repeated at the end of Section 2. |
| *"Related work is a list of papers."* | A **comparison table**; prose organised **by assumption, not by paper**. |
| *"Why does this matter?"* | **Heilmeier Q4 as its own paragraph**: who, what decision, what changes. |
| *"The evaluation is vague."* | **Baselines and datasets named**; metrics **derived from the goal**; **threats listed**. |
| *"This is not feasible in the time."* | **Milestone plan with a go/no-go gate**, compute estimate and buffer. |
| *"No risks are considered."* | **Risk table** with mitigations and **fallback contributions**. |

## Each fix is a tool from this week

The table is mostly a summary of the lecture:

- the **one-sentence claim** is the *question* level from *Topic, Problem and Question*,
  stated so it can be wrong;
- the **comparison table** is the gap-finding table, reused as the structure of the related
  work;
- **Q4 as its own paragraph** is Heilmeier and FINER's *Relevant*: name the person and the
  decision;
- the **go/no-go gate** is Heilmeier Q8, and the stop rule in the hypothesis;
- **fallback contributions** are the characterization fallback from the hypothesis example.

The evaluation fix ties back to earlier weeks. Metrics **derived from the goal** means
choosing the measure because it answers your question, not because it is customary. That is
Week 4's "statistics serve the question". **Threats listed** means naming the threats to
validity from Week 3 before a reviewer does.

## How to use it

Before you hand in a proposal, read it as the reviewer would and go down the left column. For
each comment, find the sentence, table or paragraph in your draft that answers it. If you
cannot point to one, the reviewer will not find one either.

> Every common reviewer complaint has a known fix. Apply them before someone else has to ask.
