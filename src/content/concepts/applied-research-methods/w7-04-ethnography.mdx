To understand why a team's code reviews feel hostile to newcomers, you could ask people. Or
you could join the team for a year and learn the unwritten rules yourself. The second is
**ethnography**.

## What it involves

The researcher examines an **entire group that shares a common culture**:

- usually for a **lengthy period**;
- in the group's **natural environment**, which is called **site-based fieldwork**;
- with help from **primary informants**, members who explain the group to the researcher.

The focus is the group's **everyday behaviour**, from which the researcher identifies its
**cultural norms, beliefs, practices and social structures**. In a software setting that might
mean who is allowed to merge, how disagreement is expressed, and what earns respect.

## Observer or participant

The researcher's role ranges from **passive observer** to **willing participant**. A
participant sees more from the inside but is more likely to become part of what they study.
Either way, the data are **extensive field notes** and **ongoing journals with reflections**.

**Autoethnography** turns the method inward. The researcher studies their *own*
perspectives, beliefs, experiences and practices. An example is a developer writing a
systematic account of their own first year in a company's culture.

## Analysis

| Feature | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Collection and analysis happen **somewhat simultaneously** | You interpret as you go, and what you notice changes what you look for next |
| **Description → analysis → interpretation** | First say what happens, then find the patterns, then say what they mean |
| **Rigorous subjectivity** | The researcher's view is part of the data. Instead of claiming to be neutral, you are disciplined and open about where you stand |

*Rigorous subjectivity* is the key phrase. Ethnography does not claim a neutral outsider's
view. It requires you to be systematic about a view that is admittedly your own. The
reflective journal is where that discipline lives, and *Credibility and Trustworthiness*
this week names the general strategy, **reflexivity**.

## The report

Often written in a **personal, literary style**, it contains an introduction giving the
**rationale**, a description of the **setting and methods**, the **analysis** of the group
studied, and a **conclusion**.

> Ethnography learns a culture by living in it, then writes it down honestly, including
> where the writer stood.
