Why choose a qualitative study at all, given that it cannot show cause and effect? Because
some questions are not about cause, and some are too early for a hypothesis. The lecture
lists six things a qualitative study can do.

| Purpose | What it gives you | A computing example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Exploration** | Initial insights into a topic or phenomenon | How do hobbyists decide which open-source projects to contribute to? |
| **Multifaceted description** | The complex nature of the area of focus | What does on-call actually involve for a small SRE team, hour by hour? |
| **Verification** | A test of claims or theories in real-world settings | Does "pair programming spreads knowledge" hold in a fully remote team? |
| **Theory development** | New concepts and theoretical perspectives | A model of how trust in an AI coding assistant forms and breaks down |
| **Problem identification** | The key problems uncovered | What really blocks junior developers in their first month? |
| **Evaluation** | A judgement of how effective a policy, practice or innovation is | Is the new incident-review process doing what it was meant to? |

## Reading the list

Two of these are about **starting** a line of research. Exploration and problem
identification are how you find out what is worth measuring. They connect back to Week 6:
a qualitative study is one way to find the gap that a research problem needs.

Two are about **understanding** something in its full complexity. Multifaceted description
and theory development produce an account or a model, not a number.

Two are about **checking** something that already exists. Verification tests a claim;
evaluation judges a practice. Both happen in the real setting, not a controlled one. That is
their value, because the claim meets all the messiness a lab would remove.

None of the six is "prove that X causes Y". When you write a proposal, state which of these
purposes your study serves. It tells a reviewer what kind of conclusion to expect, and stops
you promising one the method cannot deliver.

> Use a qualitative study to find, describe or check something, not to prove a cause. Say
> which of the six you are doing.
