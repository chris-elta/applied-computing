Supervised learning needs a label on every training example, and labels are expensive.
Unlabelled data is often plentiful. **Semi-supervised learning** uses a **small amount of
labelled data** and a **large amount of unlabelled data** together.

The lecture's example is **medical image classification**. A hospital has thousands of scans,
but only a few have been labelled, because labelling needs a radiologist's time. Training on
the few labelled scans alone would waste most of the data. Ignoring the labels entirely would
waste the expertise that went into them.

## Why unlabelled data helps

Unlabelled examples carry no answers, but they show the **shape of the data**: where examples
cluster and where the gaps between groups fall. The labelled examples say which group is
which. Together they can place a boundary better than the labelled examples could alone.

That only works if both sets come from the **same population**. That is the first con below,
and it is easy to miss.

## Pros and cons

| Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- |
| **Reduces labelling cost** | **Assumes unlabelled data follows the same distribution as labelled data** |
| Often achieves **better performance than unsupervised** learning | More complex than purely supervised or unsupervised methods |
| Useful when **labelled data is scarce** | **Sensitive to incorrect labels** |

## The two cons that matter for research

**Same distribution.** If the labelled scans come from one hospital and the unlabelled ones
from another, with different equipment or patients, the shape the model learns from the
unlabelled data may not match the labels. This is Week 4's sampling concern in ML form: the
two samples must represent the same population, or the combination misleads.

**Sensitivity to incorrect labels.** With few labels, each one carries a lot of weight. A
handful of mislabelled scans can push the model's view of the whole unlabelled set in the
wrong direction. In a fully supervised set of 100,000 examples, a few errors are diluted.
Here they are amplified.

For a research design, both cons become checks to plan for. Say how you will confirm the two
sets are comparable, and how the labels will be verified, for example with a second labeller
on a sample.

> Semi-supervised learning stretches a few labels across many examples. That works only if
> the labels are right and the examples come from the same place.
