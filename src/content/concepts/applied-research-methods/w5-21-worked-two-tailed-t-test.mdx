**The problem.** A jeweller's design calls for **0.08 ounces** of gold per charm. Does the
average charm actually contain 0.08 oz? A sample of **16 charms** gives the following values
(oz):

`0.0773 0.0779 0.0756 0.0792 0.0777 0.0713 0.0818 0.0802 0.0802 0.0785 0.0764 0.0786 0.0776 0.0793 0.0755 0.0806`

Test at the **0.01** level.

## Step by step

| Step | Working |
| --- | --- |
| **Parameter** | `μ` = true mean gold content of this type of charm |
| **Hypotheses** | `H₀: μ = 0.08`, `Hₐ: μ ≠ 0.08` |
| **Significance level** | `α = 0.01` |
| **Summary statistics** | `n = 16`, `x̄ = 0.07798`, `s = 0.002514` |
| **Test statistic** | `t = (0.07798 − 0.08) / (0.002514/√16) = −0.00202 / 0.0006285 ≈ −3.2` |
| **Degrees of freedom** | `15` |
| **p-value** | **Two-tailed**: one tail for `t = 3.2` at 15 df is about 0.003, so `p = 2 × 0.003 = 0.006` |
| **Compare** | `0.006 < 0.01` |

(The slide prints the sample mean as 0.077891; computing it from the sixteen values gives
0.07798, which is what yields the slide's `t = −3.2`.)

## Conclusions

- **Statistical:** reject `H₀`.
- **Contextual:** at the 0.01 level there is sufficient evidence that the true mean gold
  content of this type of charm is **not** 0.08 oz.

## A sharper statement

The lecture adds a point that goes beyond 'different'. When a two-sided test rejects the null,
the *direction* of the sample tells you which way the difference lies. Here `x̄` is below
0.08 and `t` is negative, so the jeweller can say there is convincing evidence that the true
mean is **less than** 0.08 oz.

## Checking your reading

- Two tails because the question was 'is it 0.08?', with no suspected direction.
- n = 16 falls in the 15 to 40 range, so the check from the assumptions concept asks for no
  large outliers; the charms' lowest value, 0.0713, is the one to inspect, but nothing is extreme enough to alarm.
- Even at `α = 0.01`, a strict level, the result is rejected, so it is strong evidence by
  the standards used in this course.

> A negative t is not a mistake. It is the test telling you which way the sample missed.
