Three practical aids cut the time a search takes.

## Librarians

Their job is to help you and others find information. Consult one **early**: they save you
time and frustration in finding unfamiliar and valuable resources, and they teach you how to
use resources and access databases. You can see them face-to-face or by email or chat.

## Search tactics

| Tactic | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Use **at least two keywords** | One word returns a flood; two start to describe your topic |
| Put a **phrase in quotation marks** | The engine matches it as written instead of word by word |
| Look for **government documents** and professional-organisation publications | Often free, and often the primary source for applied questions |
| Use **Wikipedia** for general overviews | It is not peer-reviewed and must not be used as an authoritative source |

The Wikipedia line is a distinction between a *map* and a *source*. A good overview helps you
learn the vocabulary and find the real papers; it is not itself something you cite as
evidence.

## Other researchers' reviews

A literature review written by someone else is a shortcut to what is important. The lecture's
procedure:

1. **Track down references cited by three or more** other researchers.
2. **Go to the original source** and read it whenever possible.
3. **Beware of authors misrepresenting** the work of a particular researcher.

Step 3 is the reason for step 2. Claims drift as they are passed along, and a summary is
somebody's reading.

> Use other people's reviews to find the papers, then read the papers.
