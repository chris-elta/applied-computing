Beyond splitting and bias, the lecture lists six issues an ML study has to plan for.

| Issue | The question it raises |
| --- | --- |
| **Overfitting and underfitting** | Has the model learned the pattern, or the noise, or neither? |
| **Model complexity and interpretability** | Can you explain *why* it predicts what it does? |
| **Computational resources and scalability** | Can you afford to train it, and will it work at full size? |
| **Feature selection** | Which inputs should the model use? |
| **Hyperparameter tuning** | How will you choose the settings, and on what data? |
| **Ethical considerations and transparency** | Who could be harmed, and can others see how it was done? |

## Overfitting and underfitting

These are the two ways a model can fail to **generalise**, meaning work well on new data.

| | Training performance | New-data performance | What happened |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Overfitting** | Very high | Much lower | Learned the training examples, noise included |
| **Underfitting** | Low | Also low | Too simple to capture the pattern |
| **Good fit** | High | Close to training | Learned the pattern |

You can only see this if you have separate data to compare with, which is one reason for the
validation set in *Training, Validation and Test Sets*. The symptom of overfitting is the
**gap**. Regularisation, simpler models and more data are the usual remedies, and the lecture
lists overfitting as a con of supervised learning for this reason.

## Complexity against interpretability

More complex models can fit more complicated patterns, and can more easily overfit. They are
also harder to explain. For research this is a real cost. If your question is *why*
something happens, a model whose reasoning you cannot inspect may answer "what" without
answering "why". It is one of the selection criteria in step 3 of the workflow.

## The practical three

- **Compute and scalability** are FINER's *Feasible* in ML terms. Estimate the compute
  before committing. *Be Your Own Reviewer* asks for a compute estimate in the milestone plan.
- **Feature selection** decides what the model is allowed to know. Too many features invites
  overfitting. A feature that secretly encodes the answer (a "discharge date" when predicting
  length of stay) is leakage.
- **Hyperparameter tuning** must use the validation set, never the test set.

## Ethics and transparency

This connects to *Bias in ML Data* and to *Planning ML Research*. A study whose methods
cannot be inspected cannot be checked or replicated. Week 1 named replication as one of the
two tests every project should pass.

> A model must fit the pattern, not the noise. You must also be able to afford it, explain
> it, and show others how it was built.
