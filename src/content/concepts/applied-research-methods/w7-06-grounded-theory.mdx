Most research starts from a theory and tests it. **Grounded theory** works the other way
round. It starts with data about something poorly understood and **builds** a theory from it.
The theory is "grounded" because every part of it can be traced back to the data.

## What makes it grounded theory

- The researcher **begins without any theoretical framework** in mind.
- The **purpose** is to construct a theory about a **little-understood** phenomenon.
- It uses a **prescribed set of procedures** for analysing data and building the model. The
  data are flexible, but the procedure is not.
- The **typical focus** is people's **actions and interactions**, including the participants'
  own perspectives on them.

## Analysis drives collection

The study **begins with data analysis**. As data come in, you categorise them and speculate
about possible concepts and themes. Data collection is **field-based, flexible and likely to
change**, and **interviews typically play a major role**.

The engine is the **constant comparative method**: **data analysis drives later data
collection**. Suppose the first five interviews keep mentioning "the assistant was right last
time". The next interviews ask about track record, and perhaps seek out people whose trust
collapsed after one bad suggestion. This is why you cannot write the full interview schedule
in advance.

## Three rounds of coding

The slide names the steps. In brief:

| Step | What it does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Open coding** | Break the data into pieces and label them, staying close to what was said | "checks the diff line by line", "trusts it for boilerplate", "burned once" |
| **Axial coding** | Relate the categories to each other: conditions, actions, consequences | Being burned once leads to line-by-line checking, unless the task is boilerplate |
| **Selective coding** | Choose the core category and organise the rest around it | Trust is calibrated by task risk |
| **Theory** | State the model the categories form | A theory of risk-calibrated trust |

Whatever form the theory takes, it is **based entirely on the data collected**.

## The report

Unlike ethnography's literary style, the writing is **objective and impersonal**. The report
gives the research question, a **review of related literature**, the methodology and data
analysis, the theory, and its implications. The literature review is no contradiction: you
start the study without a framework, but you still place the finished theory among existing
work.

> Grounded theory starts with data, not a theory. Analysis begins at once, decides what to
> collect next, and is coded in rounds until a theory emerges that rests entirely on the
> data.
