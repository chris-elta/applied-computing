Statistics are tools. The lecture's opening is a reminder of what they are for and how they
get misused.

## What statistics are

**Statistics** are **computational procedures that enable us to find patterns and meaning in
numerical data**. Three points follow from the slides:

- Data have to be **organised before they are analysed**.
- **Whatever you do to prepare the data affects the meaning that they reveal.**
- Every researcher should be able to give a **clear, logical rationale** for the procedures
  used, including whether **'cleaning up' the data** was appropriate.

The middle point is easy to miss. Removing a value, merging categories or recoding a scale is
an analytic decision, not housekeeping. It should be reported like any other.

## Describing is a beginning

Later in the deck the point returns. Statistics for **central tendency** and **variability**
are *the beginning point from which to view the data*. And then the statement that gives this
concept its name:

> Statistical manipulation of the data is not research. Research demands interpretation of
> the data.

The research question comes first. A statistic is chosen because it answers a question about
the data, not because it is available. This also ties back to Week 3's measurement scales: the
scale of your data limits which statistics are legitimate.

The next four concepts cover the main families: describing a sample, normal distributions,
the measures of centre and spread, and the inference that carries results back to the
population.
