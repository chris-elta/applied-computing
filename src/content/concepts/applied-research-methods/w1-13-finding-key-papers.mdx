There are several places to look for articles, and a way of using them that works when the
field is unfamiliar. The lecture lists where first.

## Places to look

- the **BCIT Library**: indexes, journals, books and conference papers;
- **Google Scholar**;
- **Wikipedia** — a general overview, not peer-reviewed and not an authoritative source
  (Week 2 returns to this);
- **patents**.

## A way to find the key papers

When you do not yet know who matters in an area, use the structure of the literature itself:

1. **Search generic terms** and notice which publications and authors seem to be publishing in
   the area.
2. **Look for articles that are cited a lot**, or for references that appear in many of the
   articles you find.
3. **Then look at articles that cite those popular articles.**

Step 2 takes you backwards to the foundations. Step 3 takes you forwards to what came next.
Between them you move from a bag of search hits to a map of who is arguing with whom.

Week 2 develops this into a full literature review, with databases, search strategy, record
keeping and a stopping rule.

> Citations are the literature's own index of what matters. Follow them in both directions.
