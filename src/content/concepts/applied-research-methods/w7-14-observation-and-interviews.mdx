Week 4 covered observation and interviews already, but as **quantitative** tools: a
prespecified focus, behaviour that was counted, and structured questions. The qualitative
versions use the same names and almost the opposite approach.

## Observation

In a qualitative study:

- the researcher may be an **outsider** or a **participant observer**, the same range
  ethnography uses;
- observation is **intentionally unstructured and free-flowing**;
- it **can be problematic and lack objectivity**.

| | Week 4 observation study | Qualitative observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focus | Particular and **prespecified** | Open; follow what turns out to matter |
| Record | Counts per time segment, rating scales | Field notes |
| Objectivity | Defined behaviours, trained raters, inter-rater checks | **Can lack objectivity**; supported by the trustworthiness strategies |

The lecture is candid about the cost. You notice what a fixed checklist would miss, but what
you notice depends on you. That is why reflexivity, triangulation and time on site come with
the method.

## Interviews

Qualitative interviews:

- **can yield a great deal of information**;
- tend to be **unstructured and open-ended**;
- are **not as tightly prescribed or structured** as in quantitative studies;
- tend to be **informal and friendly**;
- occasionally use **focus groups**.

The informality is deliberate. A friendly, open conversation lets people raise things you did
not know to ask about. A structured interview cannot, because its questions were fixed before
you knew what mattered.

## Focus groups

A focus group interviews **several participants simultaneously**. It can be:

| | Composition | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Homogeneous** | Alike in a way that matters, such as all on-call engineers | People speaking freely among peers |
| **Heterogeneous** | Deliberately mixed, such as engineers, managers and support staff | Seeing where perspectives clash |

A mixed group can surface disagreement, but it can also silence it. A junior engineer may not
contradict their manager in the room. *Running an Interview* comes back to this under **group
dynamics**.

> Qualitative observation and interviewing loosen control on purpose, so that you can find
> what you did not know to look for. The cost is objectivity, which has to be earned back by
> other means.
