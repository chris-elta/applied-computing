Suppose someone hands you a question — *why do some players quit our game in the first ten
minutes?* — and a week. You could collect everything ever written about player retention and
summarise it neatly. That would be a good afternoon of reading. It would not be research.

The lecture defines **research** as a *systematic process of collecting, analysing and
interpreting information* — **data** — in order to increase our understanding of a phenomenon
**and communicate what we discover**. Two parts of that sentence do the work: the process is
systematic, and there is a finding at the end that other people can read.

## What it is not

The slide lists three things that look like research and are not:

| Looks like research | Why it is not |
| --- | --- |
| Gathering information | It is information discovery or a reference skill, not a study |
| Rummaging for hard-to-locate facts | It is self-enlightenment: you learn, nobody else does |
| Moving facts from one place to another | It is documentation and organisation |

## The research cycle

The lecture's diagram has seven steps, each a short instruction to the researcher:

1. Begin with a problem or question tied to a topic of interest and concern.
2. State the **goal** of the endeavour clearly.
3. Divide the main problem into more manageable **subproblems**.
4. Identify the **assumptions** — and sometimes specific hypotheses — behind the work.
5. Develop a specific **plan** for addressing the problem.
6. **Collect, organise and analyse** data related to the problem.
7. **Interpret** the data: does it shed light on the problem, and how?

Steps 1–5 are all things you do *before* touching data. That is deliberate. Most of the later
weeks of this course fill in one of these steps: Week 1 covers 1–4, Week 3 and 4 cover 5 and
6, and Week 5 is largely about 7.

The diagram is a circle, not a ladder. Research is **iterative**: the researcher moves back
and forth between two or more steps, and step 7 is usually not the final step. Interpreting
the data tends to expose a better question, a hidden assumption or a flaw in the plan.

> Research is a loop with a finding at the end of each pass. If nothing was collected,
> analysed and reported, it was reading.
