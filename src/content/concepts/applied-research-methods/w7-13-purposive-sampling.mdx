Week 4's sampling aimed to make a sample **represent** a population, ideally by chance, so
that you could generalise and calculate the error. Qualitative studies mostly do not want
that. They want the **right** people or objects for the question.

## The principle

- The choice of sample **depends on the research question**.
- Selection of data sources **tends to be nonrandom**.
- It is **purposeful**: you choose particular people or objects because of what they can
  tell you.

Week 4 listed purposive sampling as one nonprobability design and attached a condition:
**always give a rationale**. In qualitative work it is the default, so the rationale is part
of every method section.

## Kinds of sample by design

The lecture matches particular kinds of sampling to particular designs. It names them only
briefly; the descriptions below fill in what each name means.

| Sampling | Used in | How it chooses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Purposive** | Most qualitative studies | Particular people or objects chosen for the question |
| **Theoretical** and **discriminant** | Grounded theory | Theoretical: choose the next participants to develop the emerging theory. Discriminant: choose cases that test it, to see whether it holds |
| **Primary informants** | Ethnography | Insiders who explain the group and open doors |
| **Extreme case** | Case studies | An unusual case that shows the phenomenon most clearly, such as the team with the worst incident record |
| **Convenience** | Some studies | Whoever is available |
| **Snowball** | Some studies | Each participant suggests the next. Useful for hard-to-reach groups, such as people who have reported a security flaw |

Theoretical sampling is grounded theory's constant comparison applied to people: what you
have learned so far decides who you talk to next.

## How many?

The lecture's rule: enough to provide information about the **variability that exists in
the phenomenon**. It is not a calculation, as Week 4's sample size was.

If you are studying how developers adopt a new language, and novices, veterans and people
forced into it by a team decision each experience it differently, your sample needs all
three. Twelve interviews covering that range beat forty with one kind of person. When new
participants stop adding new variation, you have probably reached enough.

> A qualitative sample is chosen for what it can teach, and is large enough when it covers
> the range of variation in the phenomenon. Always say why you chose it.
