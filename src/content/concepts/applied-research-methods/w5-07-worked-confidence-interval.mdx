**The problem.** A sample of 40 cars of one model has a **mean fuel tank capacity of 18.92
gallons**. The **population standard deviation** is taken to be **3.5** (the lecture notes
this is the unrealistic part: in practice we would not know it). Construct a **95% confidence
interval** for the mean capacity.

## Step by step

| Step | Working |
| --- | --- |
| Write the formula | `x̄ ± z* · σ/√n` |
| Fill in | `18.92 ± 1.96 × 3.5/√40` |
| Standard deviation of the mean | `3.5/√40 = 0.5534` |
| Margin of error | `1.96 × 0.5534 = 1.0847` |
| Interval | `18.92 − 1.0847 = 17.8353` to `18.92 + 1.0847 = 20.0047` |

So the interval is **(17.84, 20.00)** gallons, or (17.8353, 20.0047) before rounding.

## The two sentences the lecture wants you to write

- *95% of all possible confidence intervals for a sample size of 40 will contain the
  population mean.*
- *We are 95% confident that the mean fuel capacity of this model is between 17.8 and 20.0
  gallons.*

Both are statements about the **procedure**, and the next concept explains why the
distinction is important.

## Checking your work

- The interval should be **centred on the sample mean** (the midpoint is 18.92 ✓).
- It should be much **narrower than the spread of individual values**: σ = 3.5 for single
  cars, but the margin for the mean is only about 1.08, thanks to the `√40`.
- If you change only the sample mean, the interval slides but its width stays the same.

> Write the formula, fill it in, compute the margin, then add and subtract. Then read the
> result as a statement about the method.
