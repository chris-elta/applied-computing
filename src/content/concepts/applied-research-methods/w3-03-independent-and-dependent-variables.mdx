Every design in this week is built from the same few pieces. The first is the **variable**: a
quality or characteristic in the investigation that takes **two or more values**.

The lecture then defines the **cause-and-effect relationship** as *how much one variable
(the cause) influences another (the effect)*, and gives the two roles:

| Role | Definition | In an A/B test of a checkout page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Independent variable** | The variable the researcher studies as a possible cause, and directly manipulates | Which page version the user sees |
| **Dependent variable** | The variable potentially influenced by the independent one; it depends on it | Whether the user completes the purchase |

The names are easy to swap. A quick check: ask *which one did I choose or change, and which
one did I wait to see?* The one you chose is independent; the one you watched is dependent.

Note the word **manipulates**. In an experiment, you set the independent variable. In
correlational research and ex post facto designs (later this week), you cannot, so
"independent variable" means only "the one we suspect is the cause". That is why those
designs support weaker claims.

A study may have more than one independent variable. That is a *factorial* design, also
covered later this week.

> Independent: what you change. Dependent: what you measure. The whole experiment is the
> question of whether the second follows the first.
