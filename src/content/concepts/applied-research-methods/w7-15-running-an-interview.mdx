Week 4's interview guidelines carry over, but a qualitative interview is looser and longer,
so the guidelines matter more. Without a fixed script, the interviewer has more chances to
steer the answers.

## Before

- Identify **general interview questions** and possible **follow-up subquestions** in advance.
  This is general, not a script.
- Consider how participants' **cultural backgrounds** might influence their responses.
- Make sure your sample includes people who will give you **the kinds of information you are
  seeking**. This is *Purposive Sampling* applied to interviews.
- Find a **suitable location**.
- Get **written permission**.

## During

| Guideline | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **Establish and maintain rapport** | People only give detailed, honest accounts to someone they are at ease with |
| **Focus on the concrete and actual** | "Walk me through the last outage" yields evidence; "how do you feel about outages in general?" yields opinions |
| **Don't put words in people's mouths** | A leading question gets agreement, not information |
| **Record responses verbatim** | Your paraphrase is already an interpretation |
| **Keep your reactions to yourself** | A raised eyebrow tells the participant which answers you want |
| **Remember you are not necessarily getting the facts** | You are getting an account, shaped by memory and by you |
| **In a focus group, take group dynamics into account** | Who talks first, who defers, and who stays silent all shape what is said |
| At the **end**, ask for **additional comments** | The most useful remark often comes after "anything else?" |

## A leading question, fixed

| Leading | Open |
| --- | --- |
| "The migration was badly managed, wasn't it?" | "How did the migration go, from where you sat?" |
| "Did the lack of documentation frustrate you?" | "What did you do when you got stuck?" |

The open versions are also more **concrete**. They ask about what happened rather than
inviting a judgement.

## Recording and remote interviews

- **Audio and video recordings** from tablets, smartphones and camcorders, downloaded to a
  computer. These are what make verbatim records possible.
- **Voice-recognition software** for transcription. The lecture's example is HyperTRANSCRIBE.
- **Internet-based** interviews: Zoom, Skype and other video conferencing; **chat rooms or
  bulletin boards** for focus groups.

Recording needs the permission obtained beforehand. That is why written permission comes
first on the list.

> In a qualitative interview you are part of the instrument. Ask openly about concrete
> events, record exactly what is said, and keep your own views out of the conversation.
