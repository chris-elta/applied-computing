The matrix form assumed every constraint was a `≤` and every variable was `≥ 0`. Real LPs
have equality constraints, `≥` constraints, and variables that are **free**, meaning they
may take any real value. The dual still exists. Two things change, and both follow from
re-asking what each requirement was for.

The primal is a maximisation and the dual a minimisation throughout.

## Each primal row gives a dual variable

The multiplier's job is to turn its row into a valid `≤` statement that can join the sum.
Choose its sign so the weighted row gives an upper bound.

| primal constraint | dual variable | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `≤` | `yᵢ ≥ 0` | a negative number would reverse it |
| `=` | `yᵢ` free | an equation stays true when multiplied by anything |
| `≥` | `yᵢ ≤ 0` | a negative number reverses it into a `≤` |

For example, `x₁ ≥ 4` times `−2` is `−2x₁ ≤ −8`, a `≤` statement, which is what the sum
needs.

## Each primal variable gives a dual row

The dual row's job is to guarantee "objective term ≤ weighted term" for that variable.

| primal variable | dual constraint | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `xⱼ ≥ 0` | `(Aᵀy)ⱼ ≥ cⱼ` | with `xⱼ ≥ 0`, a larger coefficient gives a larger term |
| `xⱼ` free | `(Aᵀy)ⱼ = cⱼ` | `xⱼ` can be positive or negative, so the coefficients must match |

## What does not change

- Objective coefficients and right-hand sides trade places: `c` becomes the dual's limits and
  `b` its objective.
- The coefficient matrix is transposed.
- One dual variable per primal row, one dual row per primal variable.

## The same thing in symbols

The slide writes the general case with index sets. `I` is the set of `≤` rows, `E` the
equality rows, and `N` the non-negative variables. There are `m = |I| + |E|` constraints and
`n` variables. It covers `≤` and `=` rows. The `≥` case is in the rules table above.

| primal (max `∑ cⱼxⱼ`) | dual (min `∑ bᵢyᵢ`) |
| --- | --- |
| `∑ⱼ aᵢⱼxⱼ ≤ bᵢ` for `i ∈ I` | `yᵢ ≥ 0` for `i ∈ I` |
| `∑ⱼ aᵢⱼxⱼ = bᵢ` for `i ∈ E` | `yᵢ` free for `i ∈ E` |
| `xⱼ ≥ 0` for `j ∈ N` | `∑ᵢ aᵢⱼyᵢ ≥ cⱼ` for `j ∈ N` |
| `xⱼ` free for `j ∉ N` | `∑ᵢ aᵢⱼyᵢ = cⱼ` for `j ∉ N` |

Each line pairs one feature of the primal with one feature of the dual. The dual sums run
over `i`, down a column of the coefficients, which is the transpose again.

## The common slip

Rows pair with variables, and variables pair with rows. An equality **row** makes a
**multiplier** free. A free **variable** makes a dual **row** an equation. The two "free ↔
equals" pairings look alike and are easy to cross.

> For a max primal: a `≤`, `=` or `≥` row gives a multiplier that is `≥ 0`, free or `≤ 0`.
> A non-negative or free variable gives a dual row that is `≥` or `=`. Each rule is whatever
> keeps the weighted sum a valid upper bound.
