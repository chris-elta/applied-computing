Work at depth `k` is `(3/2)ᵏ × O(n)`. Look at the two ends of the tree:

- **Top** (`k = 0`): work is `O(n)` — the root splitting and recombining once.
- **Bottom** (`k = log₂ n`): work is `O(3^log₂n) = O(n^log₂3)`.

That last equality is the one algebraic step worth doing by hand: take `log₂` of both sides
and each comes to `log₂n · log₂3`.

Between those endpoints the work **increases geometrically**, by a factor of `3/2` each level
down.

So what does the sum over all `log₂ n` levels come to?

## The trap

The tempting answer is "`log₂ n` levels times the largest level", which would introduce an
extra log factor. That is wrong, and it is worth knowing why before reading the right answer.

Ask instead what *kind* of series this is. It is **geometric** — each term is a fixed multiple
of the one before — and geometric series with ratio above 1 do not behave like `count ×
largest`. They are dominated by their final term.

## The sum

```
Σ (k = 0 to log₂n) (3/2)ᵏ · O(n)  =  O( (3/2)^log₂n · n )  =  O(n^log₂3)  =  O(n^1.59)
```

**Why the last term is enough.** For any ratio `r > 1`, the sum of a geometric series is less
than `r/(r−1)` times its last term. With `r = 3/2` that factor is 3 — so the whole tree costs
at most three times its bottom level, and a factor of three disappears inside the `O`.

> **The leaves dominate.** The bottom level alone already costs `O(n^1.59)`, and everything
> above it adds only a constant factor.

## What was actually bought

`O(n^1.59)` against schoolbook's `O(n²)`. Kolmogorov's conjecture — that `n²` was necessary —
broken by dropping one subproblem out of four.

Note what did *not* change: the subproblems are the same size, the combine step is still
linear, the tree is still `log₂ n` tall. The single difference from the four-product version
is the branching factor, 3 instead of 4 — and that alone moved the exponent from 2 to 1.59.

Which raises the obvious question: is there a formula that reads off the answer from `a`, `b`
and `d` without drawing the tree each time? That is the next module.
