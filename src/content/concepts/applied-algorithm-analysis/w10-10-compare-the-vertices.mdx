The vertex theorem turns an LP with two variables into a short procedure:

1. **Find the vertices.** Intersect pairs of boundary lines and keep the intersections that
   satisfy every constraint.
2. **Evaluate** the objective at each one.
3. **Pick the best.**

## Step 1: finding a vertex

A vertex is where two boundary lines meet. For the corner between the Deluxe limit and the
total limit, solve both as equations:

```
x₂       = 300
x₁ + x₂  = 400
⇒  x₁    = 100
```

Then **check the remaining constraints**: `100 ≤ 200`, and both coordinates are
non-negative. So `(100, 300)` is a feasible vertex.

The check is not a formality. Intersections can lie outside the region:

| lines | intersection | kept? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `x₁ = 200` and `x₂ = 300` | `(200, 300)` | no, `500 > 400` |
| `x₁ + x₂ = 400` and `x₂ = 0` | `(400, 0)` | no, `400 > 200` |
| `x₁ + x₂ = 400` and `x₁ = 0` | `(0, 400)` | no, `400 > 300` |

Of the eight points where two of the boundary lines cross, five survive. Those are the five
corners of the region.

## Steps 2 and 3: compare

| feasible vertex | `Z = x₁ + 6x₂` | profit/day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `(0, 0)` | `0 + 6(0)` | $0 |
| `(200, 0)` | `200 + 6(0)` | $200 |
| `(200, 200)` | `200 + 6(200)` | $1400 |
| `(100, 300)` | `100 + 6(300)` | **$1900** |
| `(0, 300)` | `0 + 6(300)` | $1800 |

**Optimal plan: 100 Classic and 300 Deluxe, earning $1900 a day.** This finishes the
argument from the week's opening concept. `(100, 300)` is a feasible plan worth $1900, and
the vertex theorem says nothing beats the best vertex, which is the upper bound.

## And whole cookies

The LP allowed fractions, but its optimum `(100, 300)` is whole. Because the integer optimum
is at most the LP optimum, and this integer plan reaches the LP optimum, it solves the
whole-cookie problem too.

## The limits of the method

This works because there are few vertices. With `n` variables and many constraints, the
number of vertices can grow exponentially, and listing them all is hopeless. Simplex keeps
the vertex idea but moves between neighbouring vertices without listing them all.

> Find each vertex by solving two boundary equations, then check the remaining
> constraints. Discard intersections that fail. Evaluate the objective at the survivors,
> and the best one is the LP optimum.
