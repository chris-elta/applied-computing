BFS needs, at every step, *the vertex marked longest ago*. A **queue** hands that over in
one step. Items join at one end and leave from the other, so the item that has waited
longest is always the next out. Since all of layer 1 joins the queue before any of layer 2,
all of layer 1 leaves before any of layer 2 — the queue produces the layers by itself.

Queue operations must take `O(1)` time, which a linked queue or a circular buffer gives.

## The pseudocode

Input: a graph in adjacency-list form and a start vertex `s`.

```
1  mark s as explored, all other vertices as unexplored
2  Q := a queue data structure, initialized with s
3  while Q is not empty do
4      remove the vertex from the front of Q, call it v
5      for each neighbor w in Adj[v] do
6          if w is unexplored then
7              mark w as explored
8              add w to the end of Q
```

| lines | what happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1–2 | `s` is marked and waits in the queue |
| 4 | take the vertex that has waited longest |
| 5–8 | each unexplored neighbor is marked and joins the queue |

## Mark on the way in

The important design choice is *where the mark goes*: line 7, at the moment a vertex
**joins** the queue, not when it leaves.

Suppose you marked on line 4 instead. A vertex that has joined the queue but not yet left
is still unmarked, so every neighbor processed in the meantime sees it as unexplored and
adds it again. In the run below, `c` is a neighbor of both `a` and `b`. Marking on entry,
`a` adds `c`, and when `b` scans `c` it is already marked and skipped. Marking on exit, `b`
would add `c` a second time.

Marking on entry means **each reachable vertex joins the queue exactly once** — the fact
the running-time count on the next page stands on.

## Tracing it

Slide 30 steps through the six-vertex graph, scanning neighbors alphabetically:

| remove | scan | marked and queued | queue after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `s` | `a`, `b` | `a`, `b` | `a b` |
| `a` | `c`, `s` | `c` (`s` skipped) | `b c` |
| `b` | `c`, `d`, `s` | `d` | `c d` |
| `c` | `a`, `b`, `d`, `e` | `e` | `d e` |
| `d` | `b`, `c`, `e` | — | `e` |
| `e` | `c`, `d` | — | empty |

Marking order: `s, a, b, c, d, e`. Layer 0 is marked first, then all of layer 1, then all
of layer 2. A different neighbor order can change the marking order within a layer, and
which vertex discovers which, but never the layers themselves.

> BFS is the generic search with a queue in the open line. Mark a vertex the moment it
> joins the queue, and each reachable vertex is queued exactly once.
