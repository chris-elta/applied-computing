Take `a = (1, 2, 3)` and `b = (4, 5, 6)` and convolve them:

```
a * b = (4, 13, 28, 27, 18)
```

Read each vector as the coefficient list of a polynomial and multiply:

```
(1+2x+3x²)(4+5x+6x²) = 4 + 13x + 28x² + 27x³ + 18x⁴
```

Same five numbers. Read a vector as the coefficient list of a polynomial and convolution is
exactly the product, because both collect every `a_i b_j` with `i+j=k` into coordinate `k`.

> **Why this reframing helps.** A polynomial can be written down in two ways: by its
> **coefficients**, or by its **values** at enough points. Multiplying is slow in one
> representation and trivial in the other.

Degree check: two degree-2 polynomials give degree 4, which needs 5 coefficients — the same
`2n − 1` as the convolution length, for `n = 3` coefficients per input. Nothing about the
numbers changed by relabelling the problem; what changed is that it's now a problem about
polynomials, and polynomials have a second representation worth exploring.
