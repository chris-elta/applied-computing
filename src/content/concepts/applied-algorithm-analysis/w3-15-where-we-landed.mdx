One shape, one comparison, one picture.

## The shape

Every running time this week had the form

```
T(n) = aT(n/b) + O(nᵈ)
```

and the master theorem does exactly one thing: **compare `d` with `log_b a`.** Which side it
lands on says where the cost sits — at the root, spread evenly across the levels, or at the
leaves.

## The picture underneath

The theorem is a shortcut for an argument you can always redo by hand. Level `k` of the
recursion tree holds `aᵏ` nodes of size `n/bᵏ`, so the level costs form a geometric series
with ratio `a/bᵈ`:

- ratio below 1 — the series shrinks, the **root** is the whole cost;
- ratio exactly 1 — every level costs the same, so multiply by the `log_b n` levels;
- ratio above 1 — the series grows, the **leaves** are the whole cost.

That is why recurrences outside the theorem's form are not a dead end. Draw the tree.

## The table

| algorithm | recurrence | `a, b, d` | `log_b a` vs `d` | running time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Multiplication, four products | `4T(n/2) + O(n)` | 4, 2, 1 | `2 > 1` | `O(n²)` |
| Karatsuba | `3T(n/2) + O(n)` | 3, 2, 1 | `1.585 > 1` | `O(n^1.59)` |
| Binary search | `T(n/2) + O(1)` | 1, 2, 0 | `0 = 0` | `O(log n)` |
| Mergesort | `2T(n/2) + O(n)` | 2, 2, 1 | `1 = 1` | `O(n log n)` |
| Mergesort, quadratic merge | `2T(n/2) + O(n²)` | 2, 2, 2 | `1 < 2` | `O(n²)` |

The top two rows are the week in miniature: same `b`, same `d`, and the *only* difference is
one subproblem — which is worth `n²` against `n^1.59`.

The bottom two are the same lesson from the other direction: same `a`, same `b`, and a more
expensive combine step throws the recursion's benefit away entirely.
