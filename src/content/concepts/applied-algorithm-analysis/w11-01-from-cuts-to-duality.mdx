Last week ended with a way to prove a flow is maximum without trusting the algorithm that
found it. Two facts did the work:

```
any feasible flow value  ≤  any cut capacity
maximum flow value       =  minimum cut capacity
```

The first line means a cut is a ceiling. The second means the lowest ceiling touches the
highest flow. So a flow and a cut with equal values prove each other optimal: the flow cannot
go higher, and the cut cannot go lower.

## The pattern is not about graphs

Nothing in that argument needed vertices or edges. It needed a maximisation, a family of
upper bounds, and a guarantee that the best bound meets the best answer. This week's claim is
that **every** linear maximisation comes with such a family.

| | network flow | any linear program |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the maximisation | a flow | the **primal**: the original LP, maximise the objective |
| the upper bounds | cuts | the **dual**: the associated LP, minimise an upper bound |
| when they meet | max flow = min cut | the two optimal values are equal |

The max-flow min-cut theorem is a special case of **linear programming duality**.

## Primal and dual

The words name the two partners:

- The **primal** is the LP you were given. Here it maximises.
- The **dual** is a second LP constructed from it. It minimises, and every one of its
  feasible answers is an upper bound on every feasible answer of the primal.

The headline result, proved later in the week on the bakery example and stated in general
as the duality theorem:

> If the primal has a finite optimum, the two optimal values are equal.

The condition matters. An LP with no feasible plan, or one whose objective can grow without
limit, has no finite optimum, and the statement promises nothing about it.

## What is still missing

For flows, the upper bounds were easy to see: split the vertices and add the capacities that
cross. For a general LP there is no picture to cut. The question the next several concepts
answer is the one the slide ends on: **how do we construct this second LP?** The answer comes
from the constraints themselves, by multiplying them by well-chosen numbers and adding them up.

> A maximisation is proved optimal by a matching upper bound. For flows the bounds are cuts.
> For any linear program the bounds are the feasible answers of a second linear program, the
> dual.
