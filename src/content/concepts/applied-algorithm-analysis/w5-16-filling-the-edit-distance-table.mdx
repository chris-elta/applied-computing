The recurrence from the previous concept reads three neighbours: the cell above, the cell to
the left, and the cell diagonally up-left. Fill the base cases along the top and left edges,
and every other cell can be computed once those three are in place.

## The finished table: SNOWY against SUNNY

Rows are prefixes of `x` = SNOWY and columns are prefixes of `y` = SUNNY.

| | ∅ | S | U | N | N | Y |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **∅** | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| **S** | 1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| **N** | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| **O** | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 |
| **W** | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| **Y** | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | **3** |

## One cell, slowly

Take `E(2, 3)`: SN against SUN.

| Scenario | Candidate | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — delete `N` | `1 + E(1, 3)` | 1 + 2 = 3 |
| 2 — insert `N` | `1 + E(2, 2)` | 1 + 1 = 2 |
| 3 — `N` over `N` | `0 + E(1, 2)` | 0 + 1 = 1 |

The smallest is 1. In words: SN and SUN are one edit apart — insert the U — and the table
has found that by matching the two Ns for free.

The bottom-right cell says 3. So the edit distance is **exactly** 3: the cost-3 alignment
from two concepts back was the best possible. This is the proof the three-edit example could
not give.

## The loop

```
for i = 0…m: E(i, 0) = i
for j = 1…n: E(0, j) = j

for i = 1…m:
    for j = 1…n:
        E(i, j) = min{ 1 + E(i−1, j),
                       1 + E(i, j−1),
                       diff(i, j) + E(i−1, j−1) }
return E(m, n)
```

There are `O(mn)` subproblems with `O(1)` work each, so `O(mn)` in total.

## The grid is a dag

Each cell is a node with three edges coming in: from above, from the left, and from the
diagonal. That is the dag of subproblems from the shortest-paths concepts. Filling row by
row is one linearization; column by column is another. Either works, because in both every
arrow points forward.

## Longer words only make the rectangle bigger

EXPONENTIAL against POLYNOMIAL gives 12 rows by 11 columns — 132 cells — and an edit
distance of 6 in the bottom-right corner. Nothing about the method changes; the table has
the same shape and the count is still rows times columns.

> One cell is three lookups and a `min`. The work is in knowing what the cell means, so
> that you can check one by hand and recognise when the loop has gone wrong.
