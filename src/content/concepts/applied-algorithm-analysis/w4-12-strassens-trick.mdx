Strassen's identity computes the same four output blocks from **seven** products instead of
eight:

```
XY = [P5+P4−P2+P6   P1+P2      ]
     [P3+P4         P1+P5−P3−P7]
```

where

```
P1 = A(F−H)          P5 = (A+D)(E+H)
P2 = (A+B)H          P6 = (B−D)(G+H)
P3 = (C+D)E          P7 = (A−C)(E+F)
P4 = D(G−E)
```

Nobody derives these seven by staring at the block formula — Strassen found them in 1969, and
the search for identities with even fewer products is still an open area of research. What
matters here is only that the count drops from 8 to 7.

## Seven products, and the exponent moves

```
T(n) = 7 T(n/2) + O(n²)
```

Master theorem: `a=7, b=2, d=2`. Since `log₂7 = 2.807… > 2`, the leaves still pay, but with a
smaller exponent:

```
T(n) = O(n^log₂7) = O(n^2.81)
```

| Method | Recurrence | `a, b, d` | Running time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Definition, entry by entry | — | — | `O(n³)` |
| Block, eight products | `8T(n/2)+O(n²)` | 8, 2, 2 | `O(n³)` |
| Strassen, seven products | `7T(n/2)+O(n²)` | 7, 2, 2 | `O(n^2.81)` |

One product fewer out of eight, and the exponent falls from 3 to about 2.81 — because in the
leaf-dominated case the exponent *is* `log_b a`, so any drop in `a` moves it, however small
the drop looks. The extra additions Strassen's identity needs are `O(n²)` too, so they never
show up in the final answer — the same trade Gauss made trading a multiplication for two
additions on complex numbers, one level of the master theorem up.
