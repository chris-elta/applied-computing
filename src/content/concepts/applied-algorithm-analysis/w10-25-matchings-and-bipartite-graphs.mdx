## The puzzle

Four people on each side, and an edge between two people means they like each other:

| left | right |
| --- | --- |
| Al, Bob, Chet, Dan | Alice, Beatrice, Carol, Danielle |

Can everyone be paired with someone they like, with nobody in two pairs? That question needs
two definitions.

## Matching

A **matching** `M` in a graph `G = (V, E)` is a subset of edges, `M ⊆ E`, such that **no two
edges in `M` share a vertex**.

It pairs off some of the vertices so that every vertex is in **at most one** pair. Some
vertices may be left out. The empty set is a matching, and so is any single edge. The goal
is usually a **maximum** matching, one with as many edges as possible.

To check a proposed matching, list every vertex the chosen edges touch. If any appears twice,
it is not a matching.

## Bipartite

A graph is **bipartite**, or **two-colourable**, if its vertices can be split into two
disjoint sets `L` and `R` such that

- every edge connects a vertex in `L` to a vertex in `R`;
- no edge lies within `L` or within `R`.

The pairing puzzle is bipartite by construction: edges join a person on the left to a person
on the right. The name "two-colourable" comes from colouring `L` one colour and `R` the other,
so every edge joins two different colours.

Being bipartite is a property of the **graph**. Being a matching is a property of a **set of
chosen edges**. The flow construction in the next concept needs both.

## Perfect matching

When `|L| = |R|`, a **perfect matching** gives *everyone* exactly one partner. It has `|L|`
edges and touches every vertex.

For the opening puzzle, one exists:

| left | right |
| --- | --- |
| Al | Beatrice |
| Bob | Danielle |
| Chet | Alice |
| Dan | Carol |

Every name appears exactly once, so it is a matching, and it covers all eight people, so it
is perfect.

A perfect matching need not exist. If two people on the left like only the same one person
on the right, one of them must go unmatched. In general you cannot tell by inspection, which
is why the next step is an algorithm.

## Not only people

The same model fits anything that assigns one item to one other: people to jobs, where an
edge means the person can do that job; students to project slots; tasks to machines.

> A matching chooses edges so that no vertex is used twice. A bipartite graph splits its
> vertices into two sides with every edge going across. A perfect matching on equal sides
> gives every vertex exactly one partner.
