## The claim

For a linear objective on a **nonempty, bounded, convex polygon**:

- **at least one vertex** attains the maximum, so to find the best value it is enough to
  compare the vertices;
- there may also be optimal points along an **edge**, which a later concept covers.

The slide gives two routes to this. The picture, here, shows why it is true. The proof, in
the next concept, shows that no feasible point can beat the best vertex.

## Lines of equal profit

Fix a profit, say $600. The plans that earn exactly that satisfy

```
x₁ + 6x₂ = 600     ⟺     x₂ = 600/6 − x₁/6
```

That is a straight line with slope **−1/6**. Going right 6 Classic and down 1 Deluxe leaves
profit unchanged, because six Classic earn what one Deluxe does. Every profit value has its
own line, and all of them are parallel.

## Pushing outwards

| profit `Z` | what the line does |
| --- | --- |
| $600 | cuts through the region: many feasible plans earn $600 |
| $1200 | shifted up, still cutting through |
| $1900 | touches the region at exactly one point, `(100, 300)` |
| $2000 | misses the region entirely: no feasible plan earns $2000 |

Raising `Z` slides the line upward, parallel to itself. Feasible plans exist on the line as
long as it touches the region. The best profit is the **last contact**: the largest `Z` whose
line still meets the region. Here that is $1900, at `(100, 300)`.

Check: `100 + 6(300) = 100 + 1800 = 1900`.

## Why the last contact is a corner

A straight line sliding over a polygon with straight sides leaves it at a corner or along a
whole side. It cannot leave from the middle of a side unless the side is parallel to the
line, and then the entire side touches at once. Either way, a vertex is among the last
points touched.

This is also why the conditions matter:

- **nonempty**: there must be something to touch;
- **bounded**: otherwise the line might never leave the region, a case covered later;
- **convex**: without convexity, a line could touch the region again after leaving it.

> Every value of a linear objective is a straight line, and all those lines are parallel.
> Slide the line in the improving direction until it last touches the region. That last
> contact always includes a vertex.
