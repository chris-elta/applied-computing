Methods are picky about the shape of their input. Simplex wants every constraint as an
equation. Duality, coming next, wants every constraint as `≤`. Neither cares how *you* first
wrote the bakery. So you rewrite the LP into the form the method expects, **without changing
which plans are allowed or which is best**.

## The two forms this course uses

| | max / ≤ form | equality form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| used for | duality | simplex |
| objective | maximise | maximise |
| constraints | every one uses `≤` | every one uses `=` |
| variables | all `≥ 0` | all `≥ 0` |

They agree on two rules, maximise and non-negative variables, and differ only in the
constraints. Rewriting means fixing three things in turn:

1. the **direction** of the objective;
2. the **type** of each constraint;
3. the **domain** of each variable.

The two concepts after this one cover constraints and variables. The objective is the easy
one.

## Reversing the objective

Both forms maximise. A minimisation problem is converted by **negating the entire objective
and leaving every constraint unchanged**. The same works in reverse. For the bakery:

```
max  Z = x₁ + 6x₂     ⟺     min  −Z = −x₁ − 6x₂
```

Why the same plan wins: negation reverses order. If plan A earns more than plan B, then
`−Z(A)` is *less* than `−Z(B)`. The plan with the largest `Z` is the plan with the smallest
`−Z`.

| plan | `Z` | `−Z` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `(0, 300)` | 1800 | −1800 |
| `(100, 300)` | **1900** | **−1900** |
| `(200, 200)` | 1400 | −1400 |

**Same optimal plans, opposite objective values.** Undo the sign when you report the answer.

## Two mistakes to avoid

- **Negating the constraints as well.** They define which plans are allowed, and that must not
  change. Only the objective's sign flips.
- **Reporting the negated value.** The rewritten problem says −1900. The bakery earns $1900.

## What "equivalent" means here

Every rewrite in this section is checked by asking the same question: does each plan of the
original correspond to a plan of the new form with the same objective value, and the other
way round? If so, solving one solves the other.

> Rewrite an LP into the form a method needs by changing the objective's direction, the
> constraint types and the variable domains, while keeping the same plans. To switch
> between max and min, negate the objective only. The optimal plan is unchanged and its
> value changes sign.
