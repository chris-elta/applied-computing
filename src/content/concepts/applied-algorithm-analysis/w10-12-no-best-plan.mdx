The bakery has a best plan. Not every LP does, and there are exactly two ways for it to go
wrong.

## 1 · Infeasible: no plan at all

```
x₁ + x₂ ≤ 5
x₁ + x₂ ≥ 10
```

No pair of numbers is both at most 5 and at least 10 in total. The two half-planes do not
overlap, the feasible region is empty, and there is nothing to optimise.

## 2 · Unbounded objective: no largest value

```
maximise  3x₁ + 4x₂
subject to  x₁, x₂ ≥ 0
```

Plans exist, but none is best. The plan `(t, 0)` is feasible for every `t ≥ 0` and earns
`3t`. Take `t` large enough and it beats any proposed maximum. The profit keeps rising
without limit.

## Real, or a mistake?

| result | could be real | usually a sign of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| infeasible | demands that genuinely conflict | a `≤` typed as `≥`, or a wrong bound |
| unbounded | rare in practice | a forgotten constraint: a capacity, a demand limit |

Both are worth taking seriously, but a real bakery cannot earn infinite profit. If a model
says so, check it before believing it. In the example, the missing rules are the demand and
capacity limits.

## Unbounded region is not unbounded objective

These two are easy to confuse:

```
maximise  x₁
subject to  0 ≤ x₁ ≤ 1,   x₂ ≥ 0
```

The region is a strip, 1 wide and infinitely tall, so it is **unbounded**: `x₂` has no
upper limit. But the objective only looks at `x₁`, which never exceeds 1. So:

- the maximum exists and equals 1;
- every point `(1, x₂)` is optimal, a whole infinite edge of them;
- `(1, 0)` is a vertex, and it is among the optimal points.

What matters is whether the region extends in a direction **the objective rewards**. Here it
extends upward, and moving upward does not change `x₁`.

## The complete picture

Every LP ends in one of three ways:

| outcome | feasible region | maximum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| optimal | nonempty | attained, at a vertex if the region has one |
| infeasible | empty | none: nothing to choose |
| unbounded | nonempty and unbounded | none: values grow without limit |

> An LP either has an optimum, has no feasible plan, or has feasible plans with no upper
> limit on value. An unbounded region alone does not cause the third case. The region must
> extend in a direction the objective improves in.
