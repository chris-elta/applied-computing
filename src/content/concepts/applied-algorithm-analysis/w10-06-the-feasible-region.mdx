Before solving the bakery, separate two words that are easy to blur.

- A **feasible** solution satisfies every constraint.
- An **optimal** solution is a feasible solution with the best objective value.

Every optimal plan is feasible. Most feasible plans are not optimal.

## Four candidate plans

| plan `(x₁, x₂)` | feasible? | profit, or the rule it breaks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `(200, 200)` | yes | `200 × $1 + 200 × $6 = $1400` |
| `(100, 300)` | yes | `100 × $1 + 300 × $6 = $1900` |
| `(0, 400)` | no | 400 Deluxe exceeds the limit of 300 |
| `(200, 300)` | no | 500 cookies exceeds the total of 400 |

`(200, 300)` is the tempting one. It meets *both* demand limits exactly, and fails only the
rule that ties the two together. Checking each constraint on its own is not enough. The plan
must satisfy all of them together.

## Building the region

Each constraint allows one side of a line. The **feasible region** is the set of points
allowed by all of them at once. With two variables you can draw it, adding one constraint at
a time:

| step | constraint | keeps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `x₁ ≥ 0, x₂ ≥ 0` | the first quadrant, axes included |
| 2 | `x₁ ≤ 200` | everything left of the vertical line `x₁ = 200` |
| 3 | `x₂ ≤ 300` | everything below the horizontal line `x₂ = 300` |
| 4 | `x₁ + x₂ ≤ 400` | the side of the diagonal containing the origin |

The diagonal `x₁ + x₂ = 400` crosses the axes at `(400, 0)` and `(0, 400)`. To decide which
side to keep, test a point. The origin gives `0 + 0 ≤ 400`, so its side stays.

What survives is a five-sided polygon with corners

```
(0, 0)   (200, 0)   (200, 200)   (100, 300)   (0, 300)
```

The diagonal cuts the corner off the rectangle the demand limits would otherwise allow, and
`(200, 300)` is the corner it removes.

## Why the picture helps

The region turns "which plan is best?" into a question about a shape. The next concepts
show that the shape is always **convex**, and that the best plan can always be found at one
of its corners. For the bakery, that reduces infinitely many feasible plans to five.

> The feasible region is the intersection of every constraint's allowed side, and a plan
> must lie in all of them at once. Feasible means allowed. Optimal means the best of the
> allowed plans.
