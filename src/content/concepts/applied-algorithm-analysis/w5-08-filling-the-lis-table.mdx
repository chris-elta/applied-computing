The recipe from the previous concept, run on the lecture's input:

| `i` | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `nums[i]` | 10 | 9 | 2 | 5 | 3 | 7 | 101 | 18 |
| `dp[i]` | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 4 |

## One cell, slowly

Take `dp[5]`, the cell for the 7. The rule is: look at every earlier position holding a
smaller number, take the largest `dp` among them, add 1.

| Earlier number | Smaller than 7? | Its `dp` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | no | — |
| 9 | no | — |
| 2 | yes | 1 |
| 5 | yes | 2 |
| 3 | yes | 2 |

The best is 2, so `dp[5] = 1 + 2 = 3`. In words: the longest increasing subsequence ending
with the 7 has length 3 — `2, 5, 7` or `2, 3, 7`.

Two things to notice. The cell does not care *which* of the tied candidates it extends, only
how long they are. And the 10 and 9, although they come earlier, contribute nothing: a
larger number cannot come before 7 in an increasing subsequence.

Try `dp[6]` and `dp[7]` yourself before reading on. Every earlier number is smaller than
101, so `dp[6]` is one more than the largest cell so far: `1 + 3 = 4`. For the 18, the
smaller earlier numbers are 10, 9, 2, 5, 3 and 7 — everything but the 101 — and the best
`dp` among them is again 3, so `dp[7] = 4`.

## Reading the answer

The answer is the maximum of the row: 4. It is reached twice, at index 6 by
`[2, 3, 7, 101]` and at index 7 by `[2, 3, 7, 18]`. Here the last cell happens to hold the
maximum. That is luck, not a rule — put a 1 on the end of the array and the last cell would
be 1.

## What it costs

For each `i` the rule scans every earlier `j`. That is `0 + 1 + 2 + … + (n−1)` comparisons,
about `n²/2`, so the running time is `O(n²)`. The table itself is `n` cells.

To practise, LeetCode 300 is this problem exactly. Its follow-up asks for an `O(n log n)`
solution, which is where the next three concepts go.

> Fill one cell by hand, from the sentence that defines it, before you trust the loop. If
> you cannot say what `dp[5] = 3` means in words, the table is only numbers.
