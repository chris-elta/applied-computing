To convolve two length-`n` vectors, read them as polynomials and take the scenic route:

1. **Evaluate** both polynomials at the same `2n` points;
2. **Multiply** the two value lists pointwise — `2n` multiplications, `O(n)`;
3. **Interpolate** the `2n` product values back into coefficients.

Step 2 is nearly free. The whole cost is steps 1 and 3 — and evaluating a degree-`n`
polynomial at `2n` *arbitrary* points costs `O(n²)`, which is no better than where this
started. **Unless we get to choose the points.**

## Euler's formula

```
e^{ix} = cos x + i sin x
```

A complex number of the form `e^{ix}` sits on the unit circle at angle `x`. Multiplying two of
them adds their angles, because `e^{ix}e^{iy} = e^{i(x+y)}`. The value worth remembering:
`e^{iπ} = −1`, and `e^{2πi} = 1` — going all the way round the circle brings you back to 1.
That is what makes the next idea possible.

## Roots of unity

A **root of unity** is a complex number that yields 1 when raised to some positive integer
power. An `n`-th root of unity is a number `z` satisfying `z^n = 1`:

```
exp(2kπi/n) = cos(2kπ/n) + i sin(2kπ/n),   k = 0, 1, …, n−1
```

`n` points, evenly spaced around the unit circle, starting at 1. Write `ω = e^{2πi/n}`, and
they are `ω⁰, ω¹, …, ω^{n−1}`.

## Roots of unity in plain words

Picture a clock face of radius 1, with the number `1` sitting at 3 o'clock.

- **A point on the circle is a turn.** Multiplying by it rotates you by its angle. Multiplying
  two points adds their angles.
- **The `n`-th roots of unity cut the circle into `n` equal slices.** The first one, `ω`, is a
  turn of `360°/n`. The rest are that turn done twice, three times, and so on.
- **Do the turn `n` times and you are back at `1`.** That is all `z^n = 1` says.

For `n = 4` the slices are quarter turns: `1, i, −1, −i`. So `i` is just "turn 90°", and two
quarter turns make a half turn, which is why `i² = −1`.

Two facts follow from the picture, and the FFT needs both:

1. **Each point has an opposite.** Half a turn away from `z` is `−z`, and it is in the set too
   (for even `n`).
2. **Squaring doubles the angle.** Opposite points are 180° apart, so after doubling they are
   360° apart, the same place. The `n` points land on only `n/2` points:

```
n = 8 angles:   0°  45°  90°  135°  180°  225°  270°  315°
doubled:        0°  90°  180° 270°  0°    90°   180°  270°
```

Those four are the 4th roots of unity, so the trick repeats: `8 → 4 → 2 → 1`.
