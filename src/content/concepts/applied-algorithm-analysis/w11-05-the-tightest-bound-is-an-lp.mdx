Two requirements are in place. Multipliers must be non-negative, and their collected
coefficients must be at least 1 and 6. Many choices pass both:

| multipliers `(y₁, y₂, y₃)` | coefficient checks | upper bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `(5, 3, 6)` | `5 + 6 ≥ 1`, `3 + 6 ≥ 6` | `200(5) + 300(3) + 400(6) = 4300` |
| `(0, 5, 1)` | `0 + 1 ≥ 1`, `5 + 1 ≥ 6` | `200(0) + 300(5) + 400(1) = 1900` |

Both statements are true. Profit is at most $4300, and profit is at most $1900. But the
first tells the bakery almost nothing, and the second settles the question. A bound is worth
more the lower it is.

## Requirement 3: the tightest bound

So among all multipliers that pass the first two requirements, look for the ones with the
**smallest** value of

```
200y₁ + 300y₂ + 400y₃
```

Now read that sentence again as a problem statement. There are unknowns `y₁, y₂, y₃`. There
is a linear expression in them to minimise. There are linear conditions they must meet. That
is a linear program.

## The dual problem

```
min   W = 200y₁ + 300y₂ + 400y₃
s.t.  y₁        + y₃   ≥ 1
            y₂  + y₃   ≥ 6
      y₁, y₂, y₃       ≥ 0
```

This new LP is the **dual**. The original cookie LP is the **primal**. Each line of the dual
is one of the three requirements:

| line of the dual | the requirement it encodes |
| --- | --- |
| `y₁, y₂, y₃ ≥ 0` | 1 · keep the direction of the `≤` constraints |
| `y₁ + y₃ ≥ 1`, `y₂ + y₃ ≥ 6` | 2 · collected coefficients at least the objective's |
| `min W` | 3 · the tightest bound |

## Where each number went

Put the two programs side by side and the same numbers appear in different places.

| number in the primal | its place in the dual |
| --- | --- |
| limits 200, 300, 400 | coefficients of the objective `W` |
| objective coefficients 1, 6 | right-hand sides of the constraints |
| 3 constraints | 3 variables, one multiplier each |
| 2 variables | 2 constraints, one coefficient check each |

The question at the start of the week was how to find multipliers without guessing. The
answer is that finding the best multipliers is itself an LP, and any method that solves LPs
solves it. The guess `(0, 5, 1)` is feasible for the dual with `W = 1900`. Whether anything
does better is the next concept.

> The search for the best upper bound on an LP is another LP, the dual: one variable per
> primal constraint, one constraint per primal variable, minimising the bound.
