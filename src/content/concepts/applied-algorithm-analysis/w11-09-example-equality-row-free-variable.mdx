Here is a primal that breaks both assumptions of the matrix form: its second row is an
equation and its second variable is free.

```
max   Z = 3x₁ + 4x₂
s.t.  2x₁ +  x₂  ≤ 10
       x₁ + 3x₂  = 12
      x₁ ≥ 0,   x₂ free
```

## Building the dual in four passes

**1 · Count.** Two rows give two dual variables, `y₁` and `y₂`. Two variables give two dual
rows.

**2 · Objective.** The right-hand sides 10 and 12 become the coefficients:
`min W = 10y₁ + 12y₂`.

**3 · Dual rows, one per primal column.** Read down each variable's column, and put its
objective coefficient on the right.

| primal variable | its column (row 1, row 2) | objective coefficient | sign of variable | dual row |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `x₁` | 2, 1 | 3 | `≥ 0` | `2y₁ + y₂ ≥ 3` |
| `x₂` | 1, 3 | 4 | free | `y₁ + 3y₂ = 4` |

**4 · Signs of the dual variables, one per primal row.**

| primal row | type | dual variable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `2x₁ + x₂ ≤ 10` | `≤` | `y₁ ≥ 0` |
| `x₁ + 3x₂ = 12` | `=` | `y₂` free |

## The result

```
min   W = 10y₁ + 12y₂
s.t.  2y₁ +  y₂  ≥ 3
       y₁ + 3y₂  = 4
      y₁ ≥ 0,   y₂ free
```

| primal feature | dual consequence |
| --- | --- |
| row 1 is `≤`, row 2 is `=` | `y₁ ≥ 0`, `y₂` is free |
| `x₁ ≥ 0`, `x₂` is free | dual row 1 is `≥`, dual row 2 is `=` |

## Checking it against the theorem

The slide stops at the dual. As a check, both programs are small enough to solve by
substitution, and the duality theorem says the answers must agree.

- **Primal.** The equation gives `x₂ = (12 − x₁)/3`. Then `Z = 16 + (5/3)x₁`, and row 1
  becomes `x₁ ≤ 3.6`. Best plan: `x = (3.6, 2.8)` with `Z = 10.8 + 11.2 = 22`.
- **Dual.** The equation gives `y₂ = (4 − y₁)/3`. Then `W = 16 + 6y₁`, and row 1 becomes
  `y₁ ≥ 1`. Best multipliers: `y = (1, 1)` with `W = 10 + 12 = 22`.

Both come to 22. You can also see the certificate directly: `1 × (row 1) + 1 × (row 2)` is
`3x₁ + 4x₂ ≤ 22`, the objective on the left and the bound on the right.

## A coincidence to ignore

In this example the dual's coefficient grid looks the same as the primal's. That is only
because the two off-diagonal coefficients, `x₂` in row 1 and `x₁` in row 2, are both 1, so
swapping rows and columns changes nothing. Always read **columns**. The next example has a
grid where reading rows by mistake does not even give the right number of constraints.

> Work in a fixed order: count, write the objective from the right-hand sides, write one dual
> row per primal column, then set each multiplier's sign from its primal row. Row types set
> variable signs. Variable signs set row types.
